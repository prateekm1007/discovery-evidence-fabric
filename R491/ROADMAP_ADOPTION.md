## 3. Binding qualifications (the three places the roadmap must read through the Constitution)

1. **Art. LIX pins the attacker-iteration loop (roadmap #8 / min-path 1).** The roadmap says "iterate attacker (model/ring/prompt) against sealed bars until bars met." As written, an unqualified reading licenses tuning against the frozen benchmark. The constitutional protocol (already measured practice: R487–R488) is: **all attacker iteration happens on a DEVELOPMENT corpus; the sealed corpus is touched only by pre-registered, infrequent, committed-transport measurements** (R488: 22/22, one measurement, gate flipped to the measured state). The R487 addendum already states this verbatim ("a v4 design on a DEVELOPMENT corpus, Art. LIX; no tuning against the sealed benchmark"). The roadmap's phrase "against sealed bars" is adopted to mean *the bars stay fixed and sealed* — iteration runs on DEV. **Bars are never moved to meet the attacker** ("change the ring/model, not the bars" — Art. VII/L).
2. **Art. LXVIII pins every count in the roadmap (8/16, ≥6/16, ≥8/16, n≥5, 3 families).** Each number is a search-budget target, never an acceptance-bar floor: the first candidate and the sixteenth clear the identical bar; a shortfall is reported as a shortfall (the honest-short-list rule).
3. **Art. LXV pins the OWNER items (#7 visual billing, #10/#19 lab, #12 external review, plus the A2/behavior-deploy gating).** Owner-gated items carry `opened_at` + `escalation_count`, escalate in presentation past the threshold, and the report states the cost of non-decision rather than relabeling (the roadmap's own §4 "reachable ceiling ~7" language is the correct form and is adopted).
