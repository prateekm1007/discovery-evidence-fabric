# Economic Value Analysis — INV_V3_001

## Customer Problem
Blood pressure monitors lose accuracy over time due to calibration drift caused by environmental factors like temperature and altitude changes.

## Economic Pain
Inaccurate blood pressure readings lead to misdiagnosis, inappropriate treatment decisions, and increased healthcare costs.

## Current Cost
Patients and healthcare systems bear costs from repeated doctor visits, unnecessary medications, and complications from untreated or mistreated hypertension (EVIDENCE).

## Value Created
More accurate and reliable blood pressure measurements with reduced need for recalibration.

## Who Pays
Healthcare systems, insurance companies, and patients (for out-of-pocket costs of devices and care).

## Why They Pay
Reduced healthcare costs from fewer complications, more accurate treatment decisions, and longer device lifespan without recalibration.

## Adoption Barrier
Healthcare providers may be resistant to adopting new technology without strong evidence of improved outcomes, and patients may be price-sensitive for consumer devices.

## Value Creation Types
COST_REDUCTION, FAILURE_REDUCTION, CLINICAL_OUTCOME_IMPROVEMENT

## Quantified Benefits
- Cost reduction: Reduced costs from fewer recalibrations and fewer medical interventions due to inaccurate readings (INFERENCE)
- Failure reduction: Reduced calibration drift and improved device reliability (INFERENCE)
- Yield increase: UNKNOWN
- Throughput increase: UNKNOWN
- Energy reduction: UNKNOWN
- Service life extension: Extended device lifespan between recalibrations (INFERENCE)
- Clinical outcome improvement: More accurate diagnosis and treatment of hypertension (INFERENCE)
- Regulatory risk reduction: UNKNOWN
- Revenue opportunity: Premium pricing for more accurate devices and market share in the competitive blood pressure monitoring market (INFERENCE)
