### Mitigation Proposal

**Mechanism:**
Implementation of **Intermittent Aeration and Suck-Back (IA&SB)** to disrupt the adhesion of soluble microbial products (SMPs) and extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) to the fiber surface. This approach utilizes reverse permeation and bubble stripping to prevent the consolidation of the cake layer.

**Implementation:**
1.  **System Modification:** Replace the continuous vacuum suction pump with a valve-controlled pulsating suction system.
2.  **Cycle Protocol:** The system operates in two distinct phases:
    *   **Suction Phase (On):** Vacuum is applied for 120 seconds, allowing water to flow through the membrane.
    *   **Suck-Back/Aeration Phase (Off/Reverse):** Vacuum is released, and the blower is active (or a small positive pressure is applied) for 15 seconds. This creates a brief reverse flux ("suck-back") that detaches loosely bound SMPs/EPS, while the aeration generates micro-bubbles at the fiber surface that physically sweep away consolidated layers.
3.  **Control Logic:** An automated controller regulates the 120s/15s ratio. The net flux target is managed by adjusting the suction pressure during the On phase to maintain an average flux >18 LMH despite the higher resistance initially caused by the "off" periods.
4.  **Membrane Selection:** To ensure viability, the system must be retrofitted or designed with **Large-Bore (LB) Hollow Fibers** (e.g., 2.0 mm diameter) rather than standard small-bore fibers. Large-bore fibers have lower initial hydraulic resistance and higher tolerance to flux reversals without compaction.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Shear Stress from Aeration:** The gas-liquid shear stress ($\tau$) generated by the aeration bubbles during the Off phase must exceed the adhesive force between the consolidated SMP/EPS layer and the membrane.
*   **Permeation Pressure Balance:** The suction pressure ($P_s$) must be sufficient to maintain >18 LMH average flux over the cycle, even as the gel layer thickens. The 15-second reverse flux ensures that the net adhesion energy of the layer is reset, keeping the trans-membrane pressure (TMP) stable within operational limits for >6 months.
*   **SMP Solubility Management:** The biological selector (not part of the membrane unit but required for the MBR) must maintain SMPs in a soluble state. The IA&SB physically prevents these soluble components from *consolidating* into a rigid gel layer. The key measure is the **Reversible Fouling Index**, where the flux recovers >95% of its initial value after each 15-second aeration phase, indicating that fouling is reversible and non-structural.

### Concrete Falsification Test

**Test Name:** **Synthetic SMP Gel Layer Consolidation Under IA&SB vs. Continuous Flux**

**Procedure:**
1.  **Prepare Solution:** Create a synthetic wastewater feed containing a defined concentration of **SMPs (sodium alginate or dextran as a surrogate for soluble microbial products)** at a concentration that naturally forms a gel layer on standard small-bore membranes under continuous flux within 24 hours (to simulate rapid failure).
2.  **Baseline Run (Falsification Target):** Run a standard MBR with **continuous aeration and continuous suction** for 4 weeks. Measure the flux decline. If flux drops below 10 LMH or requires chemical cleaning to recover, the baseline failure mode is confirmed.
3.  **Proposed Run:** Run the **IA&SB system** with the same SMP feed for 4 weeks.
    *   **Cycle:** 120s Suction / 15s Aeration+Suck-Back.
    *   **Measurement:** Record the **Net Average Flux ($J_{avg}$)** and **TMP** every 24 hours.
4.  **Falsification Criteria:**
    *   The approach is **falsified** (i.e., the proposal fails) if:
        *   $J_{avg}$ drops below **18 LMH** at any point during the 4-week period and does not recover after 24 hours of continued IA&SB operation.
        *   A **chemical cleaning** (e.g., NaClO 1%) is required to restore $J_{avg}$ to >18 LMH before the 6-month mark (in a scaled-up 6-month simulation, the extrapolated cleaning frequency must exceed 6 months).
        *   **Post-run Analysis:** If SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy) or Cryo-SEM of the fiber surface after the 4-week run shows a **continuous, dense, non-porous SMP/EPS layer** thicker than 50 μm (indicating consolidation rather than loose cake), the mechanism of disruption via IA&SB is considered ineffective for this specific SMP formulation, falsifying the proposal's premise that IA&SB prevents consolidation.

**Expected Result if Proposal Works:**
*   $J_{avg}$ remains >18 LMH throughout the 4-week test.
*   TMP remains relatively stable (< 10% increase over 4 weeks).
*   No chemical cleaning is required to maintain flux.
*   Surface analysis shows a loose, porous, and easily removable cake layer, not a consolidated gel.
