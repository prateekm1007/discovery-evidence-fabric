**Mitigation Proposal: Encapsulated Inert-Gas Isolation Housing with Passive Thermal Compensation**

**Mechanism:**
The failure mechanism is driven by two external aggressors: ozone (from ambient air) causing surface cracking, and long-term viscoelastic creep under static traffic loads causing permanent deformation. To satisfy the constraint of "no replacement access after installation," the bearing must be fully isolated from environmental oxidative agents and temperature fluctuations, and its geometric stiffness must be stabilized against material degradation.

The proposed design encases the laminated elastomeric bearing pad in a sealed, rigid, stainless-steel capsule filled with a chemically inert, dry gas mixture (e.g., nitrogen with trace desiccant additives). This capsule is potted or bonded to the bridge pier and deck, eliminating any direct contact between the elastomer and ambient air. Additionally, the capsule incorporates a **passive thermal compensation layer**—a bi-material metal ring surrounding the elastomer stack—that expands/contracts differently than the steel casing, applying a variable pre-compression or tension to the elastomer as ambient temperature changes. This counteracts the stiffness drift caused by temperature-dependent modulus changes in the elastomer.

**Implementation:**
1. **Sealed Capsule Design:** The elastomeric bearing is manufactured with a 10mm clearance around its perimeter to allow free vertical movement within the capsule. The capsule is welded shut with a hermetic seal (e.g., laser-welded flange) and filled with dry N₂ gas under slight positive pressure (5 kPa) to prevent moisture ingress and oxygen diffusion. Desiccant packs (silica gel) are suspended inside the capsule to absorb any residual moisture.
2. **Passive Thermal Compensation:** The outer shell of the capsule is a bi-material assembly (e.g., Invar on the inner surface, Stainless Steel on the outer) designed such that its thermal expansion coefficient is inversely related to the stiffness drift profile of the elastomer over the expected temperature range (-20°C to +60°C). As temperature rises, the bi-material shell exerts a calibrated axial force on the elastomer stack, compensating for the drop in shear modulus.
3. **Vertical Isolation:** The capsule’s top and bottom interfaces with the deck and pier use self-centering spherical seats (PTFE-lined) that allow ±2° rotation without introducing parasitic stiffness, ensuring the vertical deflection is governed solely by the encapsulated elastomer.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Ozone/Oxygen Inerting:** Prevents surface cracking and oxidative embrittlement of the elastomer, stopping the initiation of creep damage.
*   **Moisture Control:** Prevents hydrolytic degradation of the steel-epoxy bond within the laminate, which contributes to long-term stiffness loss.
*   **Thermal Stiffness Compensation:** Directly counters the temperature-induced drift in the elastomer’s Young’s and shear moduli, keeping the effective vertical stiffness constant across seasonal cycles.
*   **Sealed Enclosure Lifetime:** The stainless-steel capsule and inert gas environment are designed to maintain hermetic integrity for ≥75 years, verified by corrosion resistance and gas tightness.

**Concrete Falsification Test:**
To falsify this approach, conduct an **Accelerated Environmental and Creep Test** on a scaled prototype (1:1 size) under the following conditions:
1. **Inert Gas Leak Test:** Fill the capsule with a tracer gas (e.g., Helium) and monitor leak rate over 1 year using mass spectrometry. If the leak rate exceeds 10% of initial tracer concentration, the inerting mechanism fails.
2. **Thermal-Load Cyclic Test:** Subject the sealed bearing to 10,000 cycles of combined thermal (-20°C to +60°C) and vertical static load (120% of design load) at a rate of 1 cycle per hour. Measure the vertical deflection under a fixed dead load (50% of design) after every 1,000 cycles.
3. **Falsification Criterion:** If the drift in vertical deflection (relative to the initial 24-hour stabilized value) exceeds **10%** at any point during the 10,000 cycles, or if visual inspection after cycling shows internal cracking or delamination via endoscopic camera through a small inspection port (sealed with a replaceable valve), the proposal is falsified. This test directly targets the constraint’s 75-year deflection drift limit and the no-replacement requirement by verifying long-term hermeticity and stiffness stability.
