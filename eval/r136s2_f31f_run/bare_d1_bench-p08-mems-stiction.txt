**Mitigation Proposal: Electrostatic De-sticking Actuation with Integrated Capacitive Sensing**

**Mechanism:**
The primary mitigation is the integration of an active, low-power electrostatic de-sticking mechanism within the comb structure itself. Instead of relying on passive mechanical separation or thermal expansion, the design employs a dedicated set of "de-stick" fingers adjacent to the main interdigitated sensing fingers. Under normal operation, these de-stick fingers are held at a low potential relative to the proof mass, maintaining a small electrostatic repulsive force or zero net force. In the event of shock-induced contact and stiction (where van der Waals forces pull the main fingers together), the control logic detects the anomaly (e.g., a sudden drop in capacitance or a shift in the null point) and triggers a high-voltage pulse to the de-stick fingers. This pulse generates a strong repulsive electrostatic force specifically designed to overcome the stiction force, ejecting the main fingers from contact and restoring the air gap. The mechanism relies on the asymmetry of the electrostatic force curve; by driving the de-stick fingers to a potential that maximizes repulsion at the specific gap closure distance, the energy barrier of the stiction state is breached.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Structural Modification:** The comb structure is fabricated with dual interdigitated arrays: a primary sensing array and a secondary de-stick array offset by a half-pitch. The de-stick fingers are made of a material with lower surface energy (e.g., fluorinated polyimide coating) to reduce baseline adhesion forces.
2.  **Control Logic:** The ASIC driving the MEMS includes a "stiction detection" state machine. It monitors the differential capacitance of the sensing array. A deviation from the nominal zero-g offset beyond a threshold (e.g., >100 mg) triggers a diagnostic routine.
3.  **Actuation Circuit:** A high-voltage charge pump (e.g., 50–100 V) is integrated onto the die. Upon triggering, a controlled pulse is applied to the de-stick fingers. The pulse width is optimized to provide sufficient kinetic energy to the proof mass to break stiction without causing excessive oscillation that would violate the zero-g offset constraint.
4.  **Zero-G Calibration:** After de-sticking, the system performs a rapid re-calibration of the zero-g offset by measuring the mid-range capacitance and adjusting the digital offset register, ensuring the residual offset remains within the 50 mg specification.

**Measures It Relies On:**
*   **Electrostatic Force Density:** The force density of the de-stick fingers must exceed the maximum stiction pressure (van der Waals + capillary forces). For typical MEMS, this requires ~10–50 mN/cm², achievable with standard 30–50 V actuation voltages.
*   **Gap Control:** The initial gap between de-stick and proof mass must be small enough (< 1 µm) to ensure strong force, but large enough to prevent contact under 10,000 g shock. This is managed via precise etch control and the constraint that the *main* fingers contact first, acting as a buffer.
*   **Energy Budget:** The charge pump must be capable of delivering the required energy in a short pulse (< 1 ms) to minimize thermal effects and power consumption, which is feasible for most MEMS ASICs.

**Concrete Test to Falsify the Approach:**
**Test Name:** Accelerated Stiction Recovery and Zero-G Offset Stability Test

**Procedure:**
1.  Subject the device to a mechanical shock of 10,000 g (half-sine pulse, 1 ms duration) along each axis to intentionally induce comb finger contact and stiction. This can be achieved using a laser-based shock rig or a calibrated ballistic pendulum.
2.  Immediately after the shock, measure the zero-g offset. Confirm it exceeds 50 mg (failure of passive design).
3.  Activate the electrostatic de-stick pulse as defined by the control logic.
4.  Measure the zero-g offset again after the pulse and subsequent stabilization.
5.  Repeat this cycle 100 times.

**Falsification Criteria:**
The proposal is falsified if:
*   **Case A:** After the de-stick pulse, the zero-g offset does not return to within 50 mg in 100% of trials. This indicates the electrostatic force was insufficient to overcome stiction or the re-calibration failed.
*   **Case B:** The de-stick pulse itself causes a permanent zero-g offset shift (e.g., due to dielectric charging, material fatigue, or electrostatic wear) exceeding 50 mg after 100 cycles. This indicates the mitigation mechanism degrades the device over time, violating the long-term constraint.
*   **Case C:** The 10,000 g shock causes structural damage to the de-stick fingers or the charge pump, rendering the actuation mechanism inoperative in >5% of trials. This indicates the mitigation is not robust under the stated shock constraint.

This test directly verifies that the active de-sticking mechanism successfully recovers the device to the required performance specification after the worst-case failure event.
