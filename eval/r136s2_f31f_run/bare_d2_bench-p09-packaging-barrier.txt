To address the degradation of the ethylene-vinyl alcohol (EVOH) layer caused by moisture plasticization while meeting the strict water vapor transmission rate (WVTR) constraint, the most robust mitigation is to modify the EVOH formulation by introducing a **low-molecular-weight plasticizer-sacrificial agent (PSA) coupled with an orthogonal silica network**, or more simply, to **coat the EVOH layer with a high-temperature, low-polarity silicone dioxide (SiO2) barrier film via sol-gel deposition or plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD)**.

Given the constraint that the failure is specifically *humidity plasticizing the polymer matrix* of the EVOH, the core issue is that EVOH absorbs water, swells, and its free volume increases, allowing oxygen and water vapor to permeate. Simply adding a standard moisture barrier outside is insufficient if the EVOH itself degrades internally. Therefore, the mitigation must prevent water from entering the EVOH matrix or lock the EVOH in a non-plasticized state.

However, the most effective and industrially standard engineering intervention for this specific failure mode (EVOH plasticization) is **incorporating a hygroscopic salt or hygroscopic polymer blend into the EVOH layer that actively scavenges moisture before it can plasticize the EVOH chain**, or, more practically, **replacing the standard EVOH with a moisture-resistant EVOH copolymer containing a hydrophobic segment and integrating it with a gas barrier interlayer (like PVDC) that is moisture-resistant**.

Let's refine this to a single, specific, and highly effective mitigation:

**Mitigation Proposal: Integration of a Hydrophobic Fluoropolymer (Fluorinated) Interlayer between the EVOH and the outer PET layer, combined with an inner EVOH layer modified with a hydrophobic silane treatment.**

Actually, a more direct and elegant solution that specifically addresses *plasticization* by humidity is to **incorporate a nano-filled hydrophobic barrier layer using hydrophobic silica nanoparticles (m-SiO2) within the EVOH matrix itself**, or to **add a second, moisture-impervious barrier layer adjacent to the EVOH that acts as a moisture sink or block**.

Let's go with the most proven industrial mitigation for EVOH plasticization: **Adding a thin, highly moisture-resistant aluminum oxide (Al2O3) layer (via PVD or CVD) on the inner surface of the EVOH layer or directly substituting the EVOH with an Al2O3-coated PET layer and using a different barrier system.** But the problem states it's a multilayer polymer film, and we want to mitigate the *existing* failure of the EVOH layer.

The best mitigation is: **Replace the standard EVOH layer with a co-extruded blend of EVOH and a moisture-resistant polyolefin (like a fluorinated polyolefin) or add a thin layer of polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) which is less sensitive to moisture plasticization than EVOH for water vapor transmission, while maintaining oxygen barrier.**

However, PVDF is not a great oxygen barrier. The key is that EVOH is excellent for O2 but poor for H2O when wet. The failure is "moisture and oxygen barrier properties... degrade".

**Revised Mitigation: Incorporate a Hydrophobic Silane-Treated Layer adjacent to the EVOH.**

Let's propose: **Co-extrude a thin layer of polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) or a fluorinated polymer (like FEP) directly adjacent to the EVOH layer on the side exposed to humidity.** PTFE has negligible water absorption and will not plasticize. It acts as a moisture shield for the EVOH. If the humidity is on the outside, place the fluoropolymer layer outside the EVOH. If the product is inside, place it inside. Since food packaging often has the EVOH in the middle, and humidity comes from the outside environment, a **thin fluoropolymer (e.g., FEP or PFA) skin layer** on the exterior of the film will block moisture from reaching the EVOH, thus preventing plasticization.

But PTFE has poor adhesion. FEP or a fluorinated polyolefin is better.

**Final Mitigation Choice:**
**Add a thin (2-5 microns) layer of fluorinated polyolefin (FPO) or polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF-F, a fluorinated variant) as the outermost layer of the film, adjacent to the EVOH layer.** This layer has extremely low water vapor transmission and does not plasticize with humidity, acting as a shield that prevents water from diffusing into the EVOH layer, thereby maintaining the EVOH's oxygen barrier and water vapor barrier properties.

**Mechanism:**
The fluorinated layer acts as a moisture-permeable-resistant shield. Since the diffusion coefficient of water in fluorinated polymers is several orders of magnitude lower than in EVOH, and fluorinated polymers do not undergo significant physical swelling (plasticization) with absorbed water, the EVOH layer remains in a dry, glassy state. This prevents the increase in free volume that causes barrier degradation.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Material Selection:** Select a fluorinated polyolefin (e.g., a copolymer of tetrafluoroethylene and hexafluoropropylene, FEP) or a fluorinated polyvinylidene (PVDF-F) with a melting point suitable for lamination/extrusion.
2.  **Process Change:** Modify the multilayer film extruder to add a new extrusion head for the fluorinated layer. Laminar this layer on the outer side of the EVOH layer (assuming external humidity attack). The layer thickness should be optimized to 3-5 microns to maintain clarity and reduce cost.
3.  **Adhesion:** Use a primer layer (e.g., a silane-coupled PET intermediate or a specific adhesive layer) if direct lamination of fluoropolymer to EVOH is difficult due to low surface energy of the fluoropolymer. Alternatively, co-extrude the fluoropolymer layer directly with the EVOH if molecular compatibility allows, or use a hot-melt adhesive compatible with both.

**Measures it Relies On:**
1.  **Water Vapor Transmission Rate (WVTR):** Must be measured at 25°C/65% RH to confirm the combined film is < 0.5 g/m²/day.
2.  **Oxygen Transmission Rate (OTR):** Must be confirmed to remain below the required threshold (not specified but implied) to ensure the O2 barrier is not compromised by the added layer.
3.  **Moisture Uptake:** Gravimetric analysis of the EVOH layer after 18 months of aging at 65% RH to confirm it remains dry (i.e., no plasticization).

**Concrete Falsification Test:**
1.  **Aging Test:** Take samples of the modified multilayer film and subject them to a 18-month accelerated aging test at 25°C and 65% relative humidity.
2.  **Measurement:** At the end of 18 months, measure the **Water Vapor Transmission Rate (WVTR)** of the film using a standard cup method (ASTM E96).
3.  **Falsification Criterion:** If the measured WVTR is **≥ 0.5 g/m²/day**, the mitigation has failed to meet the constraint.
4.  **Secondary Check (Plasticization Verification):** Additionally, perform **Dynamic Mechanical Analysis (DMA)** on the EVOH layer extracted from the aged film.
