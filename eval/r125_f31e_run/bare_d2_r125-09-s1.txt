Mitigation proposal: **Zone-Tilted Profile Control with Damper-Linked Corner Boosting**

**Mechanism:**
The uneven cure is caused by thermal stratification or heat sink effects where the corners of the load experience lower effective temperatures than the center, likely due to stagnant air zones or uneven exhaust gas recirculation. The proposed mechanism introduces a dynamic, rule-based adjustment to the oven's thermal profile that *tilts* the temperature setpoints across the existing zones specifically to compensate for corner lag. Simultaneously, it modulates the exhaust damper position to alter the internal airflow pattern, directing more heated, recirculated air toward the cold corners and reducing the "short-circuiting" of hot air directly to the exhaust. This is not a change in hardware, but a change in the control logic that correlates the existing thermocouple readings and damper position to enforce a spatial temperature gradient that neutralizes the geometric disadvantage of the corners.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Baseline Mapping:** Use the existing oven zone thermocouples to log the temperature difference ($\Delta T$) between the corner-associated zones and the center zones during the standard cycle. Identify the specific time windows in the cure phase where corner lag is most critical (typically the initial heating ramp and the soak period).
2.  **Control Law Update:** Modify the PLC logic to apply a "Corner Boost Factor."
    *   **Thermocouple Logic:** Instead of maintaining a uniform temperature across all zones, set the corner-zone thermocouple setpoints to run $N$ degrees higher than the center-zone setpoints during the heating and initial soak phases. $N$ is calculated from the historical $\Delta T$ data to ensure the *effective* part temperature at the corners matches the center.
    *   **Damper Feedback Loop:** Link the exhaust damper position to the cure timer. During the critical heating phase, command the damper to a partially closed position (relative to the baseline) to increase static pressure and recirculation, forcing air across the corners. As the cycle enters the soak phase, gradually open the damper to stabilize temperature and prevent overheating of the now-corrected corners.
3.  **Validation:** Run the modified cycle and verify hardness specs at the corners and center. Since the cycle time may no longer need to be extended to compensate for lag, the primary success metric is returning to the original cycle time while maintaining uniform hardness.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Existing Zone Thermocouples:** Used to monitor and enforce the new, non-uniform temperature setpoints and verify that the corner zones are reaching their boosted targets without overshoot.
*   **Cycle Timer:** Used as the time basis for the control law, determining *when* to apply the corner boost (heating ramp) and *when* to engage the damper modulation strategy.
*   **Exhaust Damper Position Feedback:** Used as the actuator variable to actively manipulate internal airflow dynamics. The feedback signal allows the control system to confirm the damper is in the desired position (e.g., closed enough to induce recirculation) and to adjust in real-time if position drift occurs, ensuring the airflow correction is actually being applied.
