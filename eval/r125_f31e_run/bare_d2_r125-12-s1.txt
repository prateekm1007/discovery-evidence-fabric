**Mitigation Proposal: Regeneration Cycle Optimization Based on Flow-Metered Throughput and Pressure-Adjusted Timing**

**Mechanism:**
The early hardness breakthrough and increased salt consumption suggest that the fixed-duration or fixed-volume regeneration cycles are no longer matched to the actual resin saturation rate, likely due to drift in service flow rates or changes in water quality affecting resin capacity. Since new sensors are prohibited, the mitigation relies on correlating the *existing* service flow meter data with the *existing* regeneration flow meter and inlet pressure transmitter to dynamically adjust the regeneration start trigger. Instead of a fixed time interval, the softener will initiate regeneration based on an accumulated "service flow volume" threshold that is corrected for inlet pressure variations, ensuring the resin is regenerated precisely when it reaches a predictable fraction of its exhaustion capacity, rather than at a rigid schedule that may be too early (wasting salt) or too late (causing breakthrough).

**Implementation:**
1.  **Establish Baseline Capacity Ratio:** Use historical data from the service flow meter to calculate the total service flow volume ($V_{svc}$) between two known good regenerations where breakthrough did not occur early. Determine the baseline volume-to-salt ratio ($R_{baseline} = V_{svc} / \text{Salt Used}$).
2.  **Pressure-Compensated Flow Correction:** The inlet pressure transmitter measures the force driving water through the resin bed. Higher pressure can increase flow velocity and channeling, effectively reducing the bed's apparent capacity. Implement a correction factor $P_{corr}$ derived from the inlet pressure transmitter. If pressure deviates from the nominal design pressure, adjust the expected service volume capacity inversely. For example, if pressure is 10% above nominal, reduce the target service volume threshold by 10% to anticipate faster exhaustion.
3.  **Dynamic Trigger Logic:** Modify the control logic (PLC or softener controller) to trigger regeneration not by time, but by the integral of the service flow meter reading, adjusted by the pressure correction factor.
    *   Trigger Condition: $\int_{last\ regen}^{now} Q_{svc}(t) \cdot P_{corr}(t) \, dt \geq V_{target}$
    *   Where $Q_{svc}$ is the instantaneous service flow from the service flow meter, and $P_{corr}$ is a multiplier $<1$ if pressure is high, $>1$ if low, calculated from the inlet pressure transmitter.
4.  **Regeneration Flow Validation:** After triggering, the regeneration process will use the regeneration flow meter to ensure the brine is delivered at the correct rate and volume. If the regeneration flow meter detects a deviation from the expected brine volume/time profile (indicating potential valve failure or pump issue), the system will log a fault and extend the next regeneration cycle to compensate for the incomplete regeneration, preventing immediate breakthrough.

**Measures Relied Upon:**
*   **Service Flow Meter:** Provides the critical real-time measure of water treated, allowing the system to track resin exhaustion based on actual load rather than time.
*   **Inlet Pressure Transmitter:** Provides the necessary data to correct for hydraulic variations that affect resin bed performance (channeling, velocity), enabling the adjustment of the expected capacity volume.
*   **Regeneration Flow Meter:** Validates that the regeneration process itself is performing to specification, ensuring that when a trigger is pulled, the resin is actually recharged to capacity. This closes the loop on the "salt use" aspect by confirming effective regeneration, thereby reducing the need for excessive salt dosing due to ineffective cycles.

This approach directly addresses the "hardness breakthrough arrives earlier" issue by triggering regeneration *before* breakthrough occurs based on measured load, and addresses "salt use has climbed" by preventing unnecessary early regenerations (which waste salt) and ensuring each regeneration is complete (via flow meter validation) so that the next cycle can handle more load, effectively stabilizing the salt-per-gallon-of-service ratio.
