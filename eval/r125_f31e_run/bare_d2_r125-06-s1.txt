**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Thermal Optimization and Ash-Layer-Driven Batch Scheduling**

**1. Mechanism**
The degradation is caused by an imbalance between heat input and the insulating/dross-forming effects of the bath surface. The thickening ash layer acts as an insulator and a source of reactive impurities, leading to inefficient heat transfer and increased zinc volatilization/consumption. The mechanism involves using the **Sheet-Entry Pyrometer** to detect the onset of excessive thermal resistance or bath condition changes (often correlated with surface film stability) and the **Kettle Zone Thermocouples** to assess the internal thermal profile. By correlating the production rate (from the **Production Counter**) with the thermal state (Thermocouples) and entry conditions (Pyrometer), the system identifies when the ash layer has reached a critical thickness that degrades efficiency. Instead of waiting for a weekly manual dross removal, the system triggers a **controlled "flush" or "skimming" window** by adjusting the line speed and temperature setpoints to maximize bath turnover and minimize surface residence time, forcing the accumulated ash to be mechanically removed or dissolved in a targeted, shorter cycle rather than a reactive, longer one. This optimizes the heat transfer coefficient and reduces the time the bath spends in a high-consumption, high-ash state.

**2. Implementation**
*   **Baseline Mapping:** Use historical data from the **Production Counter** and **Kettle Zone Thermocouples** to establish the nominal relationship between line speed, bath temperature, and expected zinc consumption per tonne. Identify the "healthy" ash thickness signature (often inferred from stable temperature gradients and consistent pyrometer readings at entry).
*   **Real-Time Monitoring Logic:**
    *   The **Sheet-Entry Pyrometer** measures the temperature of the sheet as it enters the kettle. Deviations from the expected thermal signature (e.g., a cooler entry due to poor heat exchange from a thick ash layer, or inconsistent readings indicating surface turbulence) serve as an early warning indicator.
    *   The **Kettle Zone Thermocouples** monitor the internal temperature uniformity. A growing delta between the top (ash layer) and bottom thermocouples, or a drop in the required heating power (inferred from temperature response to setpoint changes, if available, or simply a drift from the stable operating point) indicates increased thermal resistance due to ash.
    *   The **Production Counter** tracks the elapsed time since the last dross removal and the total tonnage processed. This provides a "wear" metric for the bath condition.
*   **Trigger for Mitigation:** Define a composite index: `Ash_Risk_Index = (Pyrometer_Deviation × Thermocouple_Gradient_Delta) + (Tonnage_Since_Last_Skim / Threshold)`. When this index exceeds a dynamically calculated threshold (based on current line speed), the system triggers the mitigation protocol.
*   **Action Protocol:**
    1.  **Speed Adjustment:** The PLC/controller reduces the line speed (via the production counter's rate setting) by 10-15% for a defined duration (e.g., 30 minutes) to increase the immersion time per unit mass, allowing for better dross floatation and reducing the entrainment of slag into the zinc.
    2.  **Temperature Surge (Optional, if safe):** If the thermocouples indicate a significant cooling trend at the surface due to ash insulation, a small, controlled increase in the target temperature (within safety limits) is applied to break the surface film and enhance dross aggregation.
    3.  **Scheduling Cue:** The system logs the timestamp and tonnage, automatically updating the "time since last skim" variable. It alerts the operator that dross removal is due *now* (replacing the fixed weekly schedule with a condition-based one). The operator performs the skim during this optimized window when the dross is most concentrated and easiest to remove, rather than when it is dispersed and thick.
    4.  **Return to Normal:** After the skim, the line speed is gradually restored to the target production rate, and the thermocouples/pyrometer are monitored to confirm the thermal profile has returned to baseline.

**3. Measures Relied Upon**
*   **Kettle Zone Thermocouples:** Used to detect changes in thermal uniformity and gradients indicative of increased surface resistance (ash layer thickness) and to verify recovery after mitigation.
*   **Sheet-Entry Pyrometer:** Used as a real-time indicator of bath heat transfer efficiency. A change in the expected thermal equilibrium at the entry point signals that the surface condition (ash/dross) is affecting process stability.
*   **Production Counter:** Used to calculate tonnage processed since the last maintenance action, providing the baseline for when the "weekly" interval is exceeded, and to adjust line speed for the mitigation protocol. It tracks the cumulative wear on the bath chemistry.
*   **No New Instrumentation:** All logic uses only the existing signals. The "Ash_Risk_Index" is a software calculation derived from these three existing sensors and the historical baseline data. The physical action (speed change, temperature adjustment) uses existing actuators. The outcome (reduced zinc consumption per tonne, less frequent but more effective dross removal) is achieved by aligning the maintenance schedule with the actual bath condition rather than a calendar-based one.
