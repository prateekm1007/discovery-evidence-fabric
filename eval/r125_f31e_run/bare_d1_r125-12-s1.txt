**Mitigation Proposal: Dynamic Regeneration Scheduling Based on Service Flow Integration**

**Mechanism**
The primary driver for increased salt consumption and earlier hardness breakthrough is likely that the regeneration cycles are currently being triggered on a fixed time interval rather than based on the actual ion exchange capacity consumed. By shifting from a time-based schedule to a capacity-based schedule, the system can regenerate the resin only when it is genuinely exhausted. This prevents premature regeneration (which wastes salt by regenerating resin that still has capacity) and delays unnecessary cycles, ensuring the resin is utilized to its maximum effective capacity before the next cycle, thereby stabilizing salt use per thousand gallons of softened water and extending the service period before breakthrough occurs.

        *   Normal Operation: Regenerate strictly when the **service flow meter** integral reaches the calculated volume limit. This ensures you only use salt when resin is actually exhausted, reducing total salt use by eliminating unnecessary "top-up" regenerations.
        *   Abnormal Operation: If the **inlet pressure transmitter** signals a significant deviation, force an immediate regeneration and log a maintenance alert for resin bed inspection, as pressure changes often precede breakthrough failures not caught by flow meters alone.

**Measures Relied Upon**
*   **Service Flow Meter:** Provides the accurate integral of water treated, which is the primary driver for capacity-based regeneration.
*   **Inlet Pressure Transmitter:** Serves as a health indicator for the resin bed. A stable pressure profile confirms the bed is not fouled or shifted. A deviation acts as an early warning for physical bed integrity issues that cause premature breakthrough, independent of water volume.
*   **Regeneration Flow Meter:** Used to verify that the regeneration brine volume meets the required backwash/rinse/salt dosing profile, ensuring the process is mechanically correct, though the trigger itself is driven by service volume and pressure health.

This approach mitigates rising salt use by eliminating unnecessary regenerations (by switching from time to volume) and mitigates earlier breakthrough by using pressure trends to detect physical bed degradation before it causes hardness spikes.
