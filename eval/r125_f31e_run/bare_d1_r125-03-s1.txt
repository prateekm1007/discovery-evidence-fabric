Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Water Adjustment via Aggregate Moisture Inference

Mechanism
Deploy a shift-to-shift adaptive control law that continuously infers the free-moisture content of incoming aggregates and automatically trims the plant water dosing to keep the concrete slump/consistency constant, despite drift.

Implementation
- Inputs (existing only): batch scale records (mass of each aggregate and cement), mixer motor current meter (time-series torque/load during mixing), plant air thermometer (ambient temperature).
- Baseline calibration: For a few initial batches at a known reference water-to-cement ratio (w/c), record the steady-state mixing current (average over the mixing cycle) and the total water metered from the plant. Use these to establish a lookup table mapping “expected mixing current” to “expected aggregate moisture” for the given aggregate mass and air temperature.
- Real-time inference: For each new batch, compute the observed mixing current signature (e.g., average current during the homogenization phase, normalized by batch mass). Compare it to the baseline lookup at the current air temperature and aggregate masses.
- Adjustment rule: Calculate a moisture correction factor ΔM = f(observed current – expected current, air temperature). Reduce or increase the plant water dose by k*ΔM, where k is a gain tuned to maintain target consistency. Log every adjustment.
- Shift continuity: Carry forward the learned correction offset across shifts; update the baseline weekly using validated slump tests from the existing quality checks.

Measures It Relies On
- Mixer current as a proxy for mixing resistance: Higher free moisture in aggregates lowers mixing torque; lower moisture raises it. The irregular current profile directly reflects inconsistent moisture.
- Batch scale records: Provide exact aggregate masses, enabling normalization of current per unit mass to remove batch-size effects.
- Plant air thermometer: Compensates for temperature-dependent water loss/evaporation and viscosity changes, reducing false drift signals.
- Existing quality-control slump tests: Used to periodically validate the current-to-moisture model and recalibrate the gain k without adding new sensors.

Outcome
By closing the loop on water dosing using only existing instrumentation, the mitigation stops the growing water additions, restores consistent mixing behavior (regular current profile), and stabilizes concrete consistency across shifts and aggregate deliveries.
