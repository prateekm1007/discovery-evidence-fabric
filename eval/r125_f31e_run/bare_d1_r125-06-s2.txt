**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop "Zinc Efficiency Index" Monitoring and Operator-Triggered Dross Maintenance Protocol**

**Mechanism:**
The failure symptoms (increasing zinc consumption, thicker ash/dross layer) indicate that the bath is accumulating impurities or that the temperature uniformity is drifting in a way that increases reaction rates with the sheet, even though the absolute setpoint is held. Since the ash layer acts as an insulator and a source of volatile loss, its thickness correlates with heat transfer efficiency and bath cleanliness. By correlating the *rate of change* of the bath temperature (using existing thermocouples) against the production throughput (production counter) and sheet surface temperature (pyrometer), we can infer the increasing thermal resistance of the ash layer and the corresponding inefficiency in zinc transfer. The mitigation is not an automatic control change, but a *diagnostic and maintenance-triggering* system that uses existing sensors to detect the drift in bath performance early, allowing operators to intervene (e.g., targeted dross skimming, fluxing, or minor temperature adjustments) before zinc consumption spikes further or the bath becomes unstable.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Data Aggregation:** Create a software routine (SCADA or historian) that pulls data every minute from:
    *   Kettle Zone Thermocouples (e.g., $T_{kettle}$)
    *   Sheet-Entry Pyrometer (e.g., $T_{sheet}$)
    *   Production Counter (e.g., $Q_{prod}$, sheets per hour or tons per hour)
2.  **Metric Calculation:** Compute a **Zinc Efficiency Index (ZEI)** defined as:
    $$ \text{ZEI} = \frac{T_{kettle} - T_{sheet}}{Q_{prod}} $$
    *   *Rationale:* As the ash layer thickens, the thermal coupling between the bath and the sheet weakens. For a given production speed ($Q_{prod}$), the pyrometer will read a cooler sheet temperature ($T_{sheet}$) relative to the kettle temperature ($T_{kettle}$) because the heat transfer is impeded by the insulating ash. Conversely, if operators try to compensate by raising $T_{kettle}$, the numerator increases. A rising ZEI indicates degraded thermal transfer efficiency due to bath surface conditions (ash/dross).
3.  **Baseline and Alarm:**
    *   Establish a baseline ZEI value during a period of known-good operation (clean bath, low zinc consumption).
    *   Set a threshold alarm (e.g., ZEI > Baseline + 10%).
    *   Set a "drift" alarm if ZEI increases by more than 2% over a rolling 24-hour window, indicating accelerating ash formation.
4.  **Operator Action Protocol:**
    *   When the alarm triggers, the system prompts the operator to perform a **targeted dross removal and fluxing cycle** rather than waiting for the weekly schedule.
    *   The protocol also recommends a small, temporary increase in $T_{kettle}$ (within existing limits) to burn off light ash, followed by immediate skimming, to reduce the insulating layer.
    *   Log the ZEI value and maintenance action to correlate and refine the threshold over time.

**Measures It Relies On:**
*   **Kettle Zone Thermocouples:** Provides the bath temperature reference ($T_{kettle}$).
*   **Sheet-Entry Pyrometer:** Measures the actual temperature of the sheet entering the bath ($T_{sheet}$), which is affected by the heat transfer efficiency across the bath surface.
*   **Production Counter:** Provides the throughput rate ($Q_{prod}$), which normalizes the thermal load. Higher production speeds increase heat loss and the rate of ash formation if the bath is not well-maintained.
*   **Existing Control System (SCADA/Historian):** To collect, process, and visualize the ZEI metric without adding new physical sensors.

This approach uses only existing instrumentation to create a sensitive, leading indicator of bath surface degradation (ash thickness/insulation), enabling proactive maintenance that reduces zinc consumption and restores efficient heat transfer, thereby mitigating the observed failure.
