**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Drive Torce Regulation via Mass Balance Feedback**

**Mechanism:**
Implement a real-time feedback control loop that dynamically adjusts the main drive motor’s torque limit (or speed reference, if operating in variable speed mode) based on the discrepancy between the input mass flow rate and the output mass flow rate. The core logic is to maintain a target "inventory" of material inside the shredder chamber. If the discharge conveyor scale indicates lower output mass than expected relative to the feed mass over a defined time window, the system infers that material is accumulating inside the shredder (screen undersize growth) or the drive is stalling on tough feed. The controller will then increase the drive power/torque setpoint to break through the stall condition, but only up to the physical limits allowed by the power meter. Conversely, if the discharge mass exceeds the feed mass (indicating potential clogging relief or sensor drift), the controller may reduce drive torque to prevent over-shredding or energy waste. This creates a self-correcting throughput system that uses the mass imbalance as a proxy for internal shredder load and stalling tendency.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Data Acquisition:** Continuously sample the feed conveyor scale (input mass flow rate, $\dot{m}_{in}$) and the discharge conveyor scale (output mass flow rate, $\dot{m}_{out}$) at a fixed interval (e.g., 1 Hz).
2.  **Mass Balance Calculation:** Compute the net mass change in the shredder system over a rolling window (e.g., 10 seconds): $\Delta M = \int (\dot{m}_{in} - \dot{m}_{out}) dt$. A positive $\Delta M$ indicates inventory buildup (potential stall/clog); a negative $\Delta M$ indicates inventory depletion.
3.  **Control Logic (PID or Bang-Bang with Hysteresis):**
    *   Define a target inventory band around zero net accumulation.
    *   If $\Delta M$ exceeds a positive threshold (indicating buildup/stall risk), increase the drive torque setpoint by a fixed increment or use a P-control term proportional to $\Delta M$.
    *   If the main drive power meter reads 90%+ of its rated capacity and $\Delta M$ is still positive, trigger a "stall mitigation" protocol: briefly pulse the drive to maximum torque for 2–3 cycles to clear tough feed, then resume normal regulation.
    *   If $\Delta M$ is negative and significant, decrease the drive torque setpoint to improve efficiency and reduce wear, as the shredder is running light.
4.  **Safety Interlock:** The new torque setpoint is clamped between the minimum operational torque and the maximum torque derived from the power meter’s capacity limit ($P_{max}/\omega$). This ensures no new hardware is required and respects existing electrical constraints.

**Measures Relied Upon:**
*   **Feed Conveyor Scale:** Provides the baseline input mass flow rate ($\dot{m}_{in}$), enabling the system to know how much material should be processed.
*   **Discharge Conveyor Scale:** Provides the actual output mass flow rate ($\dot{m}_{out}$), which directly reflects the effective throughput and confirms whether the shredder is successfully processing the feed or stalling/clogging (indicated by $\dot{m}_{out} < \dot{m}_{in}$ over time).
*   **Main Drive Power Meter:** Acts as the real-time indicator of mechanical load and stall proximity. High power readings correlate with high torque demand and tough feed. It also provides the hard limit for the control loop, ensuring the drive does not exceed its thermal or electrical ratings.
*   **Existing PLC/Controller:** No new sensors are added; only the configuration of the existing control system is updated to use these three existing signals in a new feedback algorithm. The "screen undersize growth" is implicitly corrected by ensuring the shredder does not stall (which would cause coarse output) and by maintaining consistent load, which promotes consistent particle size. If the discharge scale shows lighter-than-rated output despite high feed rates, the increased torque will ensure the shredder works harder to achieve the target particle size, thus reducing undersize and restoring proper discharge mass flow.
