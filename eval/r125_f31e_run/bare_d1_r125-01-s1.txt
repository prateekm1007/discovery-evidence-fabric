**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Screw Recovery Stabilization via Cycle-Time and Thermocouple Correlation**

**Mechanism:**
The drift in part weight and increased screw recovery time indicate that the metering phase (back-off) is becoming inconsistent or that the melt pressure/viscosity profile is changing, leading to variable shot volumes or material degradation. Since part weight is primarily determined by the shot volume (screw position) and material density, the failure mode is likely a mismatch between the programmed screw position and the actual melt state, or a creep in the screw mechanical position feedback due to wear or thermal expansion.

By correlating the **cycle-time logger** (specifically the duration of the metering/back-off phase) with **barrel zone thermocouple** trends, we can detect the onset of viscosity changes or mechanical slippage that causes the screw to travel further (increasing recovery time) or inconsistently, which directly impacts the final shot volume and part weight. The mitigation involves dynamically adjusting the **final metering pressure or metering speed profile** (or the screw position setpoint for shot volume) based on the deviation of the metering phase duration from its historical baseline, using the check-weigher data as the slow-feedback validation loop to prevent over-correction.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Baseline Establishment:** Use historical data from the cycle-time logger to establish a statistical baseline (mean and standard deviation) for the duration of the metering (screw recovery) phase at the current barrel setpoints.
2.  **Real-Time Monitoring:** Enable the controller to monitor the metering phase duration for every cycle. Calculate the deviation of the current cycle’s metering time from the baseline mean.
3.  **Thermocouple Cross-Check:** Simultaneously monitor the temperature drift in the first two barrel zones (where material heating and initial melting occur). If the metering time deviation is accompanied by a downward trend in zone 1/2 temperatures (or a specific drift pattern indicating cooler, more viscous melt), it confirms that the screw is working harder/longer due to material viscosity, not just mechanical wear.
4.  **Corrective Action:**
    *   If metering time is increasing *and* thermocouples show a cooling trend (e.g., due to ambient changes or nozzle heat loss), automatically increase the metering speed or lower the metering pressure slightly to reduce the time required for recovery, thereby standardizing the shot volume.
    *   If metering time is increasing *without* significant thermocouple drift (suggesting mechanical screw wear/slippage), the system will flag an alarm for mechanical inspection, as no process adjustment can fully compensate for significant screw backlash without risking weight variation.
5.  **Validation Loop:** The check-weigher data is aggregated every N cycles (e.g., 100 cycles). If the average part weight drifts beyond control limits despite the metering-time corrections, the system will incrementally adjust the **final screw position setpoint** for the injection shot (moving it closer to the nozzle) to compensate for the volume loss or gain caused by the varying melt pressure. This uses the check-weigher as the ultimate truth, while the cycle-time and thermocouple data provide the real-time diagnostic feedback to prevent large errors.

**Measures Relied On:**
*   **Cycle-Time Logger:** Provides high-frequency data on the duration of the metering phase, serving as the primary real-time indicator of screw recovery inconsistency.
*   **Barrel Zone Thermocouples:** Provides context for *why* the metering time is changing (viscosity vs. mechanical issue), preventing incorrect compensation.
*   **Check-Weigher:** Provides low-frequency, high-accuracy validation of part weight, allowing for slow, safe adjustments to the shot volume setpoint and confirming that the mitigation is effective without introducing new variability.
