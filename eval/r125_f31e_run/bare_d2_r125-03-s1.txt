**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Moisture-Compensated Water Control via Mixer Current Baseline Comparison**

**Mechanism:**
Implement a real-time corrective control loop that uses the mixer motor current profile as a proxy for effective aggregate moisture (and thus required water reduction). Since aggregate moisture content varies diurnally and with weather, the "same aggregate delivery" contains different free water. The mixer motor current is directly correlated with the rheological load of the mix; higher moisture in aggregates requires less added water to achieve target consistency, and vice versa. By comparing the instantaneous mixer current profile against a known-good baseline (from a reference batch with verified consistency), the system calculates the deviation and automatically adjusts the water-to-aggregate ratio for the next batch, shifting control from open-loop mass-based dosing to closed-loop rheology-based dosing.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Baseline Establishment:** Using the existing **mixer motor current meter**, record and store the normalized current-time profile for a batch produced under optimal conditions (target slump/consistency, verified by quality check). This profile serves as the "Golden Standard" signature for the current aggregate type and moisture state.
2.  **Real-Time Monitoring & Error Calculation:** For each subsequent batch, the **batch scale records** confirm the exact mass of dry aggregate and cement charged. Simultaneously, the **mixer motor current meter** streams the live current profile during the mixing phase. A local controller (PLC) computes the integral deviation (e.g., area under the curve difference or RMS difference) between the live profile and the Golden Standard baseline, scaled by the known torque characteristics of the mixer.
3.  **Moisture-Inferred Water Correction:** The controller interprets a *lower* peak/average current than baseline as "excess moisture in aggregates" (mix is already wetter) and triggers a *reduction* in the water meter for the next batch. Conversely, *higher* current indicates "dry aggregates," triggering an *increase* in water. This adjustment factor is applied specifically to the water dosing setpoint, not the aggregate setpoint.
4.  **Environmental Drift Compensation:** Integrate the **plant air thermometer** reading into the control algorithm. Since concrete consistency and mixer viscosity are temperature-dependent, the baseline current profile is dynamically adjusted for ambient temperature. A lookup table or linear model maps the air temperature to a "temperature-corrected" baseline current value, ensuring the moisture-inference step remains valid across shifts with varying weather conditions.
5.  **Feedback Loop:** The adjusted water setpoint is sent to the existing water metering system. The process repeats for the next batch, allowing the system to self-correct for gradual moisture drift in aggregate deliveries without manual intervention.

**Measures Relied On (All Existing):**
*   **Mixer Motor Current Meter:** Used as the primary sensor for inferring mix rheology/moisture deviation. Its non-invasive nature and direct correlation with mix workability make it ideal for closed-loop consistency control.
*   **Batch Scale Records:** Provide the accurate mass inputs (aggregate, cement) necessary to normalize the current signal (current is dependent on batch size and material mass). Without this, the current profile could not be meaningfully compared across batches.
*   **Plant Air Thermometer:** Provides the environmental variable needed to de-trend the baseline. It allows the controller to distinguish between a current change caused by temperature (which affects viscosity) versus a change caused by moisture (which affects water demand), ensuring the water adjustment is only triggered by moisture-related deviations.
