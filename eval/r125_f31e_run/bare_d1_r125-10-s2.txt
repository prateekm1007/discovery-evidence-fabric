**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Drive Torce Limiting Based on Real-Time Mass Balance**

**Mechanism:**
The degradation in performance—evidenced by increased screen undersize (fines), lighter discharge flow, and drive stalling—is indicative of excessive internal residence time or choking within the shredder chamber, likely due to material buildup or inconsistent feed density. Since new sensors are prohibited, the system will utilize the existing mass balance (Feed Scale - Discharge Scale) and the Main Drive Power Meter to create a dynamic, protective torque limit that automatically reduces the shredder's aggression (or effectively reduces the load it attempts to process) when it detects incipient stalling or mass accumulation, thereby restoring stable throughput without requiring hardware changes.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Calculate Net Mass Accumulation Rate:** The control system continuously computes the difference between the feed conveyor scale rate ($R_{in}$) and the discharge conveyor scale rate ($R_{out}$). A positive net accumulation ($R_{in} > R_{out}$ over a short window) indicates material is building up inside the shredder, increasing resistance.
2.  **Detect Stall Precursor via Power Signature:** The main drive power meter is used to monitor torque demand. The system establishes a baseline "healthy" power profile for a given load. A sharp rise in power combined with a drop in rotational speed (derived from power/voltage/frequency if VFD data is available, or simply high power at low load) or a sustained high power draw without proportional discharge mass increase serves as the stall precursor trigger.
3.  **Dynamic Torque/Power Limit Adjustment:**
    *   **Normal Operation:** The drive operates at its standard rated torque limit.
    *   **Protective Mode:** If $R_{in} > R_{out}$ persists for $T_{delay}$ OR if drive power exceeds a threshold $P_{th}$ (calibrated as 90% of the stall threshold) for $T_{delay}$, the controller *temporarily reduces* the drive’s maximum allowable torque/power limit. This reduction is not a stop, but a "softening" of the drive’s resistance to load, allowing it to slip slightly or operate at a lower effective torque to clear the blockage/fines buildup without stalling.
    *   **Recovery:** Once $R_{out}$ recovers and the mass balance stabilizes ($R_{in} \approx R_{out}$), and power draw normalizes, the torque limit gradually ramps back up to the rated value.

**Measures Relied On:**
*   **Feed Conveyor Scale:** Provides $R_{in}$, the incoming mass flow rate.
*   **Discharge Conveyor Scale:** Provides $R_{out}$, the actual processed mass flow rate. The difference $(R_{in} - R_{out})$ is the primary indicator of internal material accumulation (choking/fines buildup) which causes the increased resistance leading to stalling.
*   **Main Drive Power Meter:** Provides real-time load feedback. It validates whether the drive is struggling (high power) relative to the output, confirming the mechanical cause of the throughput drop. It acts as the immediate safety trigger to prevent hard stalls that could damage the drive.
*   **Existing VFD/Drive Parameters:** The mechanism assumes the variable frequency drive (VFD) or motor controller supports dynamic adjustment of torque limits or power caps, which is a standard software feature on industrial drives, not a new sensor.

**Rationale:**
This approach directly addresses the symptom (stalling due to buildup) by using the existing mass balance to detect the *cause* (accumulation) before the drive fully stalls. By proactively reducing the torque limit when accumulation is detected, the shredder is allowed to "shed" load rather than fight it, preventing the hard stall. As the fines/accumulation clears (indicated by $R_{out}$ rising to match $R_{in}$), normal high-torque operation resumes. This uses only the installed scales and power meter, complying with the no-new-instrumentation constraint.
