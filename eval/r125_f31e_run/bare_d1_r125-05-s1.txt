Mitigation: Implement a closed-loop fineness control using the existing mill differential pressure transmitter to modulate the feed rate.

Mechanism: The flattening of the mill’s internal pressure signature indicates a reduction in the density of the grinding bed (air load), which correlates with larger particle sizes and lower specific surface area. In a closed-circuit cement mill, the differential pressure across the mill body is a proxy for the particle size distribution and the air entrainment; as particles become finer, turbulence and air flow resistance change. However, a *flattened* signature typically suggests the mill is not fully loaded or the material is moving too quickly (under-loading) or the separator is passing material that is still too coarse to create the expected back-pressure, leading to lower specific surface. To restore the target specific surface without adding new sensors, we must use the differential pressure (DP) as the primary process variable. By correlating DP with the target fineness (using historical data from the existing setup), we can infer when the fineness is dropping. The mitigation involves adjusting the mill feed rate inversely to the DP deviation to maintain the critical bed depth and pressure signature. If DP drops (flattens), the grinding bed is likely thinning or the material is coarse, so we should *reduce* the feed rate to allow the remaining material to be ground finer, thereby restoring the pressure signature and increasing the specific surface area.

Implementation:
1.  **Baseline Calibration:** Use the existing mill-feed rate meter and DP transmitter data to establish a stable operating point where the DP is at the target value and the specific surface is at goal. Record the relationship between DP and throughput under these ideal conditions.
2.  **Control Logic Adjustment:** Modify the existing feed rate control loop. Use the DP transmitter reading as the error signal for fineness control. If the DP falls below the setpoint (indicating the flattened signature and potential coarse material/lower bed depth), automatically reduce the mill feed rate via the mill-feed rate meter control system. This allows the grinding bed to deepen (as feed slows down relative to grinding capacity) and the particles to be ground finer, which will restore the DP signature and increase specific surface.
3.  **Stabilization:** Once the DP returns to the target setpoint, gradually increase the feed rate back up to the maximum permissible level that maintains the DP, thereby restoring throughput. This prevents the over-grinding that reduces efficiency while ensuring the fineness target is met.

Measures Relied On:
*   **Mill Differential Pressure Transmitter:** Used as the primary indicator of the grinding bed density and particle fineness. The "flattened signature" is detected as a deviation from the historical DP trend associated with good fineness.
*   **Mill-Feed Rate Meter:** Used to actuate the control variable. The mitigation relies on adjusting the feed rate in response to DP changes to control the load on the mill and thus the fineness.
*   **Existing Measurements/Historical Data:** The control logic relies on the established correlation between DP and specific surface from previous campaigns, as no new instrumentation is allowed. The "flattened signature" is identified by the DP transmitter's trend, and the corrective action is purely a modulation of the feed rate based on this existing sensor input.
