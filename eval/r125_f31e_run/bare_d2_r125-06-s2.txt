**Mitigation Proposal: Ash-Layer-Compensated Dipping Time Control**

**Mechanism**
The accelerating formation of zinc oxide (ash) acts as an insulating layer between the bath surface and the hot, reactive metal bath below. While the kettle thermocouples confirm that the bulk molten zinc temperature is stable, this ash layer increases thermal resistance and reduces the effective heat flux at the surface. This leads to a lower effective temperature at the zinc-slab interface, slowing the wetting reaction and increasing the time the sheet spends in the bath to achieve a satisfactory coating thickness. Longer dipping times expose the sheet to more atmospheric oxygen, exacerbating ash formation and dross buildup, thereby creating a positive feedback loop that increases zinc consumption. The mitigation uses the *Sheet-Entry Pyrometer* to infer the thermal state of the bath surface (proxy for ash thickness) and dynamically adjusts the *dipping time* (via the production counter logic) to compensate for reduced surface reactivity.

**Implementation**
1.  **Baseline Mapping:** Calibrate a lookup table (LUT) correlating *Sheet-Entry Pyrometer* readings at the bath surface with the *Dipping Time* required to achieve the target coating weight. The pyrometer measures the radiance of the molten zinc surface; a clean bath emits at a characteristic temperature (e.g., 450°C), while a thick ash layer attenuates radiance, causing the pyrometer to read a lower "apparent" temperature.
2.  **Real-Time Adjustment:** The control system continuously monitors the *Sheet-Entry Pyrometer* value. If the reading drops below a set threshold (indicating increased ash thickness/insulation), the system automatically increases the *Dipping Time* for subsequent sheets to ensure adequate coating adhesion. Conversely, if the reading is high (clean bath), the *Dipping Time* is reduced to the minimum required.
3.  **Dross Removal Trigger:** Integrate the *Production Counter* with the *Pyrometer* trend. Instead of a fixed weekly schedule, trigger a dross removal maintenance stop when the *Sheet-Entry Pyrometer* reading falls below a critical minimum (indicating excessive ash layer) *or* when the *Dipping Time* has been increased by >X% from baseline for more than Y hours. This ensures dross is removed precisely when it negatively impacts process efficiency, rather than on a calendar basis.

**Measures Relied Upon**
*   **Sheet-Entry Pyrometer:** Used as the primary sensor to estimate the insulating effect of the ash layer by measuring surface radiance. A drop in pyrometer reading correlates with increased ash thickness, which is the root cause of the reduced wetting efficiency and increased required dipping time.
*   **Production Counter:** Used to track cumulative sheet count and time intervals, enabling the shift from time-based to condition-based dross removal. It also logs the duration of adjusted dipping times to validate the correlation between pyrometer readings and process drift.
*   **Kettle Zone Thermocouples:** Used as a validation sensor to confirm that the bulk bath temperature is stable, ensuring that any pyrometer reading drop is attributed to surface insulation (ash) rather than a bulk temperature loss, thus preventing unnecessary heating adjustments.
