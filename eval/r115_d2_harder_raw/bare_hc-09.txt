The primary mechanism driving accelerated capacity loss and safety risks in commercial lithium-ion battery cathodes is the cascade of structural defects initiated by bulk lattice distortion and cation mixing, which subsequently propagates into surface microcracks and lattice reconstruction. This multi-scale degradation pathway hinders lithium-ion transport and triggers severe side reactions with the electrolyte, specifically accelerating transition metal dissolution and inducing dangerous oxygen evolution under high voltage. To exploit an understanding of this mechanism, one must move toward a systematic modification scheme that integrates coating and elemental doping with particle morphology regulation and in-situ film formation. These strategies effectively stabilize interfaces and optimize material structures, thereby improving ion transport efficiency and suppressing the side reactions that lead to fading and hazards. Future advancements should focus on clarifying the atomic-level mechanisms of these defects through multi-scale collaborative modification to develop low-cost, feasible processes for high-performance energy storage.
