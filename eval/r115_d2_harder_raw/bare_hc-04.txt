The mechanism is that membrane fouling is driven by a chemical adsorption reaction between the solute and the membrane pore surface, rather than simple physical blockage. In this process, foulants adhere to the pore walls, which causes the pore radius to decrease over time. This reduction in radius increases the hydraulic resistance, leading to the observed decline in permeate flux. The non-linear sensitivity to bulk solute concentration arises because the rate of this adsorption reaction is governed by reaction kinetics (specifically a rate law dependent on foulant concentration and available pore area) rather than linearly scaling with concentration. To exploit this, you should model the time-dependent flux by coupling a kinetic reaction rate law with the Hagen-Poiseuille equation. Specifically, you define the rate of pore radius reduction as proportional to the foulant concentration raised to a specific reaction order and the available pore area. By substituting the time-dependent pore radius into the Hagen-Poiseuille law, you can derive explicit predictive equations for flux, resistance, or cumulative volume that accurately capture the non-linear concentration dependencies using a small number of fitted parameters, such as the rate constant and reaction order. This approach is superior to classical models because it accounts for the dynamic, chemically-driven shrinkage of the pores.
