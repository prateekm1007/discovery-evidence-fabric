The central hypothesis is that the simultaneous optimization of bulk lattice robustness and interfacial charge extraction is the primary mechanism for decoupling high power conversion efficiency from long-term stability in perovskite solar cells. Specifically, the study posits that introducing mixed cations creates a statistically stabilized crystal lattice that resists thermal and moisture-induced phase separation, while distinct interface modifications mitigate degradation pathways at the charge extraction layers. To exploit this mechanism, one should implement a dual-pronged engineering strategy: first, synthesize mixed-cation perovskites (such as FA/Cs or MA/FA/Cs combinations) to leverage the "statistical solid solution" effect, which broadens optical absorption and enhances crystallinity without sacrificing structural integrity; second, apply tailored interface engineering to improve the open-circuit voltage and fill factor by reducing non-radiative recombination losses at the boundaries. The observed data supports this, as the mixed-cation devices achieved the highest efficiency and retained 86.7% of their performance after 500 hours, whereas unmodified or interface-only modifications did not achieve the same balance of stability and peak performance. Therefore, exploiting this mechanism requires the integrated fabrication of mixed-cation bulk layers paired with optimized interlayers, rather than treating compositional changes and interface treatments as isolated variables.
