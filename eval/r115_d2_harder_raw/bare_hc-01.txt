Based on the findings in the abstract, the core hypothesis is that the introduction of iron atoms into the titanium dioxide lattice fundamentally alters the material's electronic structure to facilitate the electro-generation of hydroxyl radicals. Specifically, the Fe doping creates a higher concentration of active surface sites or lowers the energy barrier for the discharge of water molecules. By exploiting this, the anode becomes significantly more efficient at converting electrochemical energy into chemical oxidants. In practical application, this mechanism is exploited by operating the electrode under mild conditions (pH 7, moderate current density) where the Fe-TiO2 interface effectively promotes the formation of hydroxyl radicals. These radicals then act as the primary non-selective oxidants that attack and break down the complex molecular structure of Rhodamine B, achieving a high degradation rate of 89% compared to the inferior performance of pure TiO2.
