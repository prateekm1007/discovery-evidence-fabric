The central hypothesis is that the synergistic integration of Cerium doping into the 001-facet-exposed titanium dioxide lattice creates a dual-mechanism system that simultaneously optimizes charge dynamics and spectral responsiveness, specifically enhancing the degradation of PFOA under visible light.

To exploit this mechanism, one must leverage the specific structural advantages described in the study. First, the exposure of the 001 crystal facets provides a high density of active hydroxyl sites and anisotropic surface properties that favor the adsorption of PFOA molecules, while simultaneously creating internal electric fields that drive the rapid spatial separation of photogenerated electron-hole pairs. This prevents recombination, which is a primary limitation in standard photocatalysis. Second, the incorporation of Ce ions into the TiO2 lattice acts as an intermediate energy level within the bandgap. This narrows the effective bandgap of the composite material, allowing it to absorb lower-energy visible photons that pure TiO2 cannot utilize.

The exploitation of this synergy involves a two-step process: initial visible light absorption by the Ce-doped structure generates electron-hole pairs; these charge carriers are then rapidly separated and transferred due to the anisotropy of the 001 facets. The electrons migrate to the surface to react with oxygen and form superoxide radicals ($\cdot O_2^-$), while holes migrate to surface-bound PFOA molecules or react with adsorbed water/hydroxyl groups to form hydroxyl radicals ($\cdot OH$). These highly reactive species attack the C-F bonds and carbon backbone of PFOA, leading to its mineralization. By using the solvothermal preparation method, the material ensures good dispersion of Ce and uniform facet exposure, maximizing the surface area available for these radical-mediated degradation reactions. This approach directly exploits the "crystal facet modulation and Ce doping" synergy to achieve high efficiency (95.4% for 5 mg/L PFOA in 240 min) under simulated sunlight conditions.
