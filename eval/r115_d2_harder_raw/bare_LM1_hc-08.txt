The underlying mechanism is that the interfacial region acts not merely as a passive junction, but as an active chemical and physical mediator where electronic defect passivation, ionic kinetic barriers, and mechanical stress distribution are intrinsically coupled. Specifically, unpassivated interfacial defects lower the local activation energy for ion migration, which accelerates phase segregation and generates lattice strain that further weakens the perovskite-contact interface, creating a positive feedback loop for degradation. To exploit this, one must engineer a dual-function interfacial layer that utilizes a high-affinity chemical anchor to covalently or strongly coordinate with halide vacancies and cation sites, thereby suppressing electronic recombination and raising the energy barrier for ion migration. Simultaneously, incorporating a compliant polymer or elastomeric component into this interface allows it to act as a stress buffer, absorbing the lattice mismatch strains caused by temperature fluctuations or mechanical bending without transferring destructive shear forces to the brittle perovskite layer, thus decoupling electrical performance from mechanical failure.
