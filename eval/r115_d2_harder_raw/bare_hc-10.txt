The hypothesis is that the mechanism involves the integration of graphene into the polysulfone matrix to form a percolating conductive network, which significantly enhances the membrane's electrical conductivity. To exploit this, the membrane is operated with an in-situ applied low-voltage electrical field (e.g., 15 V). This field interacts with the charged foulants (such as proteins) and the charged surface of the modified membrane, inducing electrostatic repulsion and electrokinetic effects that delay the deposition of foulants and reduce flux decline compared to conventional filtration without electrical input.
