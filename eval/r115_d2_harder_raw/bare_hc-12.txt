The mechanism for selectively tuning the solid-state morphology of hydrothermal ferberite lies in the supersaturation dynamics and nucleation kinetics dictated by the physical state of the iron precursor, specifically $FeSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$. The hypothesis is that the solid-state morphology can be controlled by manipulating the initial concentration gradient of $Fe^{2+}$ ions available for crystal growth. When the iron precursor is introduced as a dilute solution, the low initial concentration of $Fe^{2+}$ leads to low supersaturation, which favors slow nucleation and the gradual, anisotropic growth of nanoplatelets where specific crystallographic planes grow preferentially due to surface energy minimization. Conversely, when the precursor is used in its solid form, the hydrothermal dissolution process creates a locally high concentration of $Fe^{2+}$ ions upon reaction with the acidic medium and tungstate sources. This results in a burst of high supersaturation, triggering rapid and massive nucleation. The high density of nuclei competes for available tungstate and iron ions, inhibiting the long-range anisotropic growth needed for large platelets and instead promoting the agglomeration of smaller crystallites into self-organized microspheres through secondary particle attachment or self-assembly mechanisms driven by electrostatic and steric interactions during the high-growth-rate regime.

To exploit this mechanism for optimizing visible-light photocatalytic performance without dopants, the strategy is to favor the formation of nanoplatelets. While both morphologies maintain the same stoichiometric composition and monoclinic crystal structure, the nanoplatelets offer a higher specific surface area and distinct anisotropic electronic structures compared to the dense microspheres. The thinner geometry of the platelets facilitates more efficient separation of photogenerated electron-hole pairs due to shorter charge-carrier diffusion paths to the surface, reducing recombination rates. Additionally, the large surface area exposes more active sites for the interaction with $H_2O_2$ and the adsorption of organic pollutants like methylene blue. The p-type semiconductor nature of ferberite, with a band gap of approximately 1.7 eV, allows for visible light absorption. By synthesizing the nanoplatelet morphology through the dilute solution precursor method, one maximizes the available active surface and optimizes charge dynamics, thereby achieving rapid photodegradation under simulated solar irradiation with low $H_2O_2$ concentrations, as observed in the study. This approach leverages intrinsic structural advantages rather than chemical doping to enhance photocatalytic efficiency, ensuring the sustainability and purity of the resulting ferberite photocatalyst.
