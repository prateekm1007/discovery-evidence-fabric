Based on the thermodynamic analysis provided in the paper's abstract, the stable immobilization of uricase onto the green-synthesized gold nanoparticles is primarily governed by hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces. These non-covalent interactions facilitate the formation of a stable protein-nanoparticle complex, which effectively enhances the thermal stability of the enzyme while retaining its catalytic activity. To exploit this mechanism, one can leverage the inherent stability of these interactions to develop reusable biocatalysts for biosensing and diagnostic applications. By engineering the surface chemistry of the *Ganoderma lucidum*-derived gold nanoparticles to maximize hydrogen bonding sites and surface area suitable for van der Waals contacts, it is possible to create robust biogenic nanomaterials that protect uricase from denaturation, thereby enabling efficient enzyme recovery and sustained performance in therapeutic or diagnostic devices without the need for covalent linkers that might compromise enzyme conformation.
