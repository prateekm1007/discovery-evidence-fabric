The mechanism underlying the non-linear sensitivity to bulk solute concentration is likely a transition from convective transport dominance to adsorption-driven pore blockage, where the fouling resistance is not simply proportional to the accumulated mass but depends on the surface saturation of specific pores. To exploit this, one should develop a model that couples the time-dependent flux decline with a Langmuir or LDF (Linear Driving Force) adsorption isotherm, treating the pore throat as a heterogeneous adsorption site rather than a uniform filter medium. This allows the model to capture the initial rapid flux decline as the most accessible pores saturate quickly, followed by a slower, non-linear plateau as the effective permeation area shrinks and the local concentration boundary layer thickens, thereby decoupling the flux response from simple bulk concentration scaling and instead linking it to the derivative of the adsorbed mass per unit time.
