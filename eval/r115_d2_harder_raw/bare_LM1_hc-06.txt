The core mechanism involves leveraging the specific structural and electronic modifications of the Ce-doped 001-face titanium dioxide to address the two primary limitations of visible-light photocatalysis: the high bandgap of TiO₂ and the strong electron-withdrawing nature of perfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS).

First, doping TiO₂ with cerium (Ce) introduces a new energy level within the bandgap, narrowing the effective bandgap and enabling the absorption of visible light to generate electron-hole pairs. Second, and perhaps more critically for refractory substrates like PFOA, the exposure of the (001) crystal face provides a specific surface structure that enhances the interaction with the pollutant. The hypothesis is that the Ce-doped (001)-TiO₂ facilitates a specific adsorption-degradation synergy where the exposed (001) face, modified by Ce sites, acts as a strong adsorption site for the PFOA molecule, particularly targeting the carboxyl group or the C-F bonds through Lewis acid-base interactions or radical mediation. This localized concentration of the pollutant on the active surface sites allows the photogenerated holes ($h^+$) to directly attack the C-F bonds, which are notoriously difficult to break, leading to defluorination and eventual mineralization.

To exploit this mechanism, one must synthesize the catalyst to strictly control the crystal facet exposure, ensuring a high percentage of (001) faces are present, and optimize the Ce doping concentration to balance light absorption with recombination losses. The exploitation strategy involves operating the system under visible light where the catalyst is pre-loaded with wastewater; the strong adsorption capacity of the (001) faces brings the PFOA into close proximity with the photogenerated oxidative holes and Ce-mediated active sites, thereby significantly lowering the activation energy required for C-F bond cleavage and accelerating the degradation rate compared to bulk or undoped TiO₂.
