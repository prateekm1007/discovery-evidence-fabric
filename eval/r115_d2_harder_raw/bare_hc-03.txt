The mechanism driving uncontrolled lithium loss during thermal processing is the evaporation of lithium hydroxide (LiOH) vapor. This occurs because water vapor reacts with non-volatile lithium oxide (Li₂O) to form LiOH, which then becomes volatile at high temperatures. Crucially, even trace amounts of moisture—from dry room environments or "pure" oxygen supplies—are sufficient to fuel this reaction.

To exploit this knowledge and accurately control stoichiometry, the process must focus on rigorous control of moisture. By minimizing water vapor concentration in the reaction atmosphere, the formation of volatile LiOH is suppressed, allowing for quantitative prediction and minimization of lithium loss rates. This enables the elimination of the empirical practice of adding excess lithium, leading to finer control over final cathode quality.
