The core hypothesis is that doping titanium dioxide with iron alters the electronic structure of the anode, facilitating the direct generation of highly reactive oxygen species (specifically hydroxyl radicals, ·OH) via the electro-oxidation of adsorbed water or hydroxide ions, rather than relying on less efficient pathways. The iron dopants likely serve as active sites that lower the overpotential for water oxidation, thereby increasing the flux of ·OH radicals at the electrode–electrolyte interface. To exploit this mechanism, one must optimize the iron concentration and crystal structure (likely anatase or mixed-phase) to maximize the surface area and specific active sites available for dye adsorption and subsequent radical attack. Since Rhodamine B is susceptible to hydroxyl radical degradation, the enhanced local concentration of ·OH at the iron-doped TiO₂ surface accelerates the oxidative breakdown of the dye’s chromophoric structure, leading to superior mineralization efficiency compared to pristine TiO₂ anodes.
