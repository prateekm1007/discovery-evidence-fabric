**Mitigation Proposal: Wind-Shearing-Compensated Oil Temperature Monitoring via Dynamic Baseline Shift**

**Mechanism:**
The upward temperature trend at rated wind is likely not due to a sudden catastrophic failure (given unchanged vibration and recent servicing), but rather a cumulative drift caused by a mismatch between the thermal load and the cooling capacity under specific aerodynamic conditions. The core issue is that the control system or maintenance team is likely comparing the absolute oil temperature against a fixed threshold, ignoring the fact that the *heat load* on the gearbox is not constant even at "rated" power; it varies with the *wind shear profile* and *turbulence intensity*, which are not fully captured by the mean nacelle anemometer reading.

The proposed mitigation implements a **post-hoc, sensor-fusion-based dynamic baseline correction**. It uses the existing nacelle anemometer (to capture mean wind speed and, crucially, the *fluctuation* or turbulence intensity over time) and the existing vibration sensors (to capture high-frequency mechanical stress that may not yet manifest as "changed signature" in standard RMS values but correlates with heat generation) to create a *real-time, expected temperature model*. The "failure" signal is then redefined not as the absolute temperature, but as the **residual error** between the *measured* oil temperature and the *predicted* temperature based on the current wind and vibration state.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Data Preprocessing (Existing Sensors Only):**
    *   **Nacelle Anemometer:** Calculate the root-mean-square (RMS) of the wind speed fluctuations over a rolling 10-minute window. This serves as a proxy for **Turbulence Intensity (TI)**, which significantly impacts gearbox thermal load via varying torque demands and oil flow dynamics.
    *   **Vibration Sensors:** Extract the high-frequency energy (e.g., 20–100 Hz band, excluding the main gear mesh frequencies) from the existing vibration channels. This captures "background noise" or early-stage frictional heating that contributes to oil temperature rise but is not yet visible in the standard low-frequency "vibration signature" used for routine health checks.
    *   **Oil Temperature Probes:** Use the standard probe readings.

2.  **Baseline Model Construction (Offline/Online Hybrid):**
    *   Using historical data from periods of confirmed *healthy* operation (before the trend started), build a simple linear regression model:
        `T_oil_expected = β0 + β1*(P_turbine) + β2*(TI_wind) + β3*(VIB_highfreq) + β4*(Ambient_Temp)`
        *   `P_turbine` is derived from SCADA power data (existing measurement).
        *   `TI_wind` is the calculated RMS fluctuation from the nacelle anemometer.
        *   `VIB_highfreq` is the band energy from vibration sensors.
        *   `Ambient_Temp` is from the existing ambient sensor (implied as standard existing measurement).
    *   This model is *not* a new sensor; it is a computational layer using *existing* measured quantities.

3.  **Real-Time Residual Monitoring:**
    *   The system continuously calculates `T_oil_residual = T_oil_measured - T_oil_expected`.
    *   The mitigation is triggered when `T_oil_residual` exceeds a statistically derived threshold (e.g., 99th percentile of historical residuals) **persistently over 1 hour**, rather than when `T_oil_measured` exceeds a fixed limit.

4.  **Automatic Mitigation Action (Control Loop Adjustment):**
    *   If a positive residual trend is detected (indicating the oil is hotter than predicted for the current wind/vibration state), the system automatically **increases the cooler fan speed by 5–10%** for the next 24 hours. This is a *temporary* compensatory action, not a permanent change.
    *   Simultaneously, it flags the unit for a *targeted* inspection of the *oil cooler heat exchanger fins* (for fouling) and *oil level* (for low oil volume), which are the most likely physical causes of a residual temperature rise that do not immediately change the vibration signature.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Turbulence Intensity (TI) as a Heat Load Driver:** It relies on the physical principle that higher TI at rated wind causes greater fluctuating torque and thus greater frictional heat in the gearbox, which is not accounted for by simple power-based thresholds.
*   **High-Frequency Vibration Band as a Thermal Proxy:** It relies on the fact that early-stage bearing/gear surface degradation or misalignment generates high-frequency vibration energy that dissipates as heat *before* it alters the fundamental gear-mesh vibration signature. Thus, the "unchanged vibration signature" is a *low-frequency* observation; the *high-frequency* component is the missing link.
*   **Existing Sensor Fusion:** It strictly uses only the specified sensors: nacelle anemometer (for TI), vibration sensors (for high-frequency energy), and oil temperature probes (for measurement). No new hardware is added. The mitigation is purely an algorithmic re-interpretation of existing data, enabling a more sensitive and context-aware failure detection.
