**Mitigation Proposal**

**Mechanism:**
Re-tune the existing air-fuel ratio (AFR) control loop to target a slightly higher O2 setpoint within the safe operating range, thereby reducing the excess air and improving heat transfer efficiency. The rising stack temperature and degraded economizer approach indicate that excess air is being heated unnecessarily, carrying heat out of the system without contributing to steam generation. By optimizing the oxygen trim based on the existing O2 analyzer and fuel flow meter, we can reduce the volume of combustion gases, lowering the stack temperature and improving the economizer approach, which directly reduces fuel consumption per tonne of steam.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Baseline Verification:** Confirm that the O2 trim analyzer and fuel flow meter are calibrated and functioning correctly using existing maintenance records. Do not add new sensors.
2.  **Control Logic Adjustment:** Access the boiler’s existing control system (DCS/PLC). The current strategy likely maintains a fixed high O2 setpoint or a wide control band to ensure complete combustion, which is inefficient.
    *   Calculate the current excess air percentage using the existing O2 measurement and theoretical O2 for the specific fuel.
    *   Implement a feedforward correction using the existing fuel flow meter: Link the O2 setpoint dynamically to the steam load (indicated by fuel flow, as load is steady). At steady load, reduce the O2 setpoint to the minimum value that maintains non-zero O2 (typically 2-4% for natural gas, or per manufacturer’s minimum for the specific fuel), ensuring no CO formation (use existing stack temperature trends and O2 noise as indirect health indicators; if available, rely on the absence of visible flames or known CO limits from previous calibrations, but strictly using existing O2 trim).
    *   *Constraint Compliance:* This uses only the O2 trim analyzer and fuel flow meter for the control decision. No new instrumentation is added. The "measures it relies on" are the existing O2 concentration readings and fuel mass flow rates to adjust the damper or VFD speed of the forced draft fan (if the fan speed is controlled; if not, adjust the damper position via the existing control valve/fan damper actuator, which is part of the existing system).
3.  **Economizer Approach Monitoring:** Monitor the existing stack thermocouples and feedwater thermocouples. A reduction in excess air should lower the stack gas temperature. As the stack temperature drops, the temperature difference between the flue gas and the feedwater in the economizer will increase, improving heat transfer (approach). If the feedwater outlet temperature (or economizer outlet water temperature, if a thermocouple exists) rises, it confirms the improved heat recovery.
4.  **Fuel Consumption Verification:** Monitor the existing fuel flow meter. As the O2 setpoint is optimized down (reducing excess air), the fuel flow meter should register a lower fuel flow for the same steam production (steady load), directly reducing fuel per tonne of steam.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **O2 Trim Analyzer:** Provides real-time measurement of excess air in the stack gas. This is the primary feedback signal for optimizing the air-fuel ratio.
*   **Fuel Flow Meter:** Provides the actual fuel input rate. This is used for two purposes: (1) To calculate the air-fuel ratio and ensure the O2 setpoint is appropriate for the specific fuel type, and (2) To monitor the reduction in fuel consumption as a direct outcome of the mitigation.
*   **Stack Thermocouples:** Used to verify the reduction in stack gas temperature, which is the physical mechanism that improves the economizer approach. A lower stack temperature indicates less heat loss up the chimney and more heat transferred to the water in the economizer.
*   **Feedwater Thermocouples:** Used to verify the improvement in the economizer approach. If the economizer outlet water temperature (or the final feedwater temperature before the boiler drum) increases at the same steam load, it confirms that more heat is being recovered from the flue gas, validating the success of the mitigation.

This approach leverages existing sensors to optimize combustion efficiency by reducing excess air, directly addressing the symptoms of rising stack temperature, degraded economizer approach, and increased fuel consumption.
