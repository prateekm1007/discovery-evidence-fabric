**Mitigation Proposal: Real-time Specific Power Index Monitoring and Threshold Alarming**

**Mechanism:**
Since the specific energy (work per unit volume) can be approximated using the available measurements of motor power and discharge conditions, the mitigation involves establishing a continuous, automated calculation of a "Specific Power Index" (SPI) derived strictly from existing data points. This index serves as a proxy for the adiabatic efficiency of the compression process. A rising SPI at constant pressure and flow indicates increased irreversibilities (e.g., valve leakage, oil degradation, or interstage heat transfer issues) leading to the observed higher discharge temperatures and energy consumption. The system will implement a soft-alarm that triggers when the SPI exceeds a statistically derived baseline threshold, prompting maintenance action without requiring new hardware.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Data Acquisition:** The Plant DCS (Distributed Control System) or a PLC tag server will pull the following existing analog inputs at a 1-second refresh rate:
    *   $P_{dis}$: Discharge Pressure (from existing pressure transmitter, in bar absolute or gauge).
    *   $T_{dis}$: Discharge Temperature (from existing temperature transmitter, in °C or K).
    *   $P_{in}$: Suction Pressure (assumed atmospheric or available from a nearby existing gauge; if not directly measured, use a fixed baseline atmospheric pressure constant $P_{atm}$, as suction pressure variations are minimal compared to discharge changes in most plant setups, or use the existing suction gauge if available). *Note: The constraint lists "discharge pressure," so we will use a fixed reference suction pressure $P_{ref}$ (standard atmospheric or typical plant suction) to maintain strict compliance with "existing sensors" if a suction transmitter is not explicitly listed as available. However, usually, air compressors have suction gauges. We will assume the available "discharge pressure transmitter" is used in conjunction with a known/constant suction reference to keep the calculation robust. If a suction transmitter exists but wasn't listed, we would use it; if not, we use the standard $P_{atm}$ as the lower bound.*

    *   $V_{flow}$: Delivered Volumetric Flow. *Wait, the constraint says "existing sensors... discharge pressure and temperature transmitters and the compressor motor power meter". It does NOT list a flow meter.*
    *   **Correction on Flow:** The problem states "at the same delivered pressure and flow." This implies flow is a known operating point or can be inferred. However, the constraint explicitly lists *only* pressure, temp, and power meters. We cannot add a flow meter.
    *   **Re-evaluating the Metric:** Without a direct flow meter, we cannot calculate specific energy per cubic meter directly ($W/V$). However, we *can* calculate **Actual Work per Unit Discharged Volume** only if we know the volume. Since we don't have a flow meter, we must look for a proxy.

If $V_{flow}$ (which is roughly proportional to $V_{suction}$) is constant (implied by "same... flow" in the problem description, meaning we are comparing performance at this operating point), then a rise in Specific Energy is equivalent to a rise in **Motor Power ($W_{motor}$)** for a given **Discharge Pressure ($P_{dis}$)** and **Discharge Temperature ($T_{dis}$)** indicates efficiency loss.
    *   **Revised Mitigation:** Implement a **Power-Pressure-Temperature (PPT) Correlation Alarm**.

Therefore, a rise in specific energy is detected by a **rise in Motor Power** at a **constant Discharge Pressure**.
    *   The mitigation is a **Pressure-Compensated Power Trending and Alarm**.

**Revised Implementation (Strictly using available sensors):**
1.  **Define Operating Envelope:** The DCS will define the normal operating range for Discharge Pressure ($P_{dis}$).
2.  **Calculate Power-to-Pressure Ratio ($R_{pp}$):** Compute $R_{pp} = W_{motor} / P_{dis}$ continuously.
3.  **Establish Baseline:** Using historical data, establish a reference curve or average value $R_{pp, base}$ for the compressor's normal efficient state.
4.  **Discharge Temperature Verification:** Use the existing $T_{dis}$ sensor to confirm the anomaly. The algorithm will flag a fault when:
    *   $P_{dis}$ is within the normal operating band (to ensure comparison is valid).
    *   $R_{pp}$ deviates above the baseline $R_{pp, base}$ by a set threshold (e.g., +5%).
    *   AND $T_{dis}$ is simultaneously above its historical average for that pressure level.
5.  **Action:** Generate a maintenance ticket tagged as "High Specific Energy / Efficiency Loss" when these conditions are met.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Statistical Baseline:** The mitigation relies on the statistical stability of the compressor's power consumption relative to its discharge pressure under normal conditions. A rise in specific energy (work per unit volume) at constant flow translates directly to a rise in total power ($W = P_{specific} \cdot V$). If $V$ is constant, $W \uparrow$ means $P_{specific} \uparrow$.
*   **Correlation Validation:** It relies on the physical principle that increased internal losses (leakage, friction, poor heat exchange) raise both the power required (lowering efficiency) and the discharge temperature (due to higher entropy generation/irreversibilities), allowing the temperature sensor to act as a corroborating filter against false alarms from other causes (e.g., ambient temperature changes affecting suction density, which would alter the Power-Pressure ratio differently than internal efficiency loss).
*   **No New Hardware:** Uses only the existing $P_{dis}$, $T_{dis}$, and $W_{motor}$ signals.
