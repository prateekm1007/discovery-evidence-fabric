**Mitigation Proposal**

**Mechanism:** Implement a **Fan-Capacity Modulation Protocol** based on a real-time "Effective Wet-Bulb Error" metric derived from existing sensors. The mechanism assumes that the widening approach temperature is partially due to the cooling tower operating at a fan speed that is no longer optimal for the current ambient conditions and fouling level. By dynamically adjusting the fan speed (or cycle time, if variable speed drives are not available, but ammeters imply monitoring of current draw which correlates to load) to maximize the heat rejection capacity relative to the ambient wet-bulb estimate, we can pull the cold-water temperature closer to the theoretical wet-bulb limit, thereby narrowing the approach.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Estimate Ambient Wet-Bulb ($T_{wb}$) and Dry-Bulb ($T_{db}$):** Use the hot-basin and cold-basin thermometers along with the make-up water meter flow rate to estimate the ambient conditions if direct weather sensors are unavailable (though typically ambient sensors are standard, the constraint says "existing sensors... hot and cold basin... fan motor ammeters and make-up water meter"). *Correction:* The prompt implies these are the *only* sensors. Therefore, we cannot directly measure ambient $T_{db}$ or $RH$. However, the **make-up water meter** provides the evaporation rate (losses). We can use the **Energy Balance Method** to infer the effective cooling performance.
    *   Let $T_h$ = Hot Basin Temp, $T_c$ = Cold Basin Temp.
    *   Let $\dot{m}_{cw}$ = Circulating water flow (assumed constant or inferred from system pressure/flow if not measured, but we have make-up water meter $\dot{m}_{makeup}$).
    *   The heat rejected $Q$ is proportional to the mass of water evaporated ($\dot{m}_{makeup}$) plus sensible heat loss (negligible for approach calculation compared to latent heat).
    *   Approach Temperature $\Delta T_{approach} = T_c - T_{wb}$. Since we don't have $T_{wb}$, we define a **Relative Approach Degradation Index (RADI)**. We compare the current $T_c$ against the expected $T_c$ for the same $T_h$ and load at the "last clean" baseline, or more practically, we maximize the ratio of Evaporation to Total Water Inflow which correlates with effective cooling capacity.
    *   *Simpler Robust Implementation without Ambient Sensors:* Use the **Hot Basin Temperature ($T_h$)** as the fixed thermal load driver and the **Make-up Water Rate ($\dot{m}_{makeup}$)** as the proxy for cooling effectiveness. As the tower fouls or fan performance drops, the evaporation efficiency per degree of approach drops.
    *   **Action:** Create a lookup table or control law that targets a specific **Make-up Water per Ton of Cooling** efficiency. However, we don't have "Tons of Cooling" directly.
    *   **Revised Implementation Logic:**
        1.  Calculate the **Current Cooling Effectiveness Index (CEI)**: $CEI = \frac{\dot{m}_{makeup} \cdot L_v}{\dot{m}_{total} \cdot C_p \cdot (T_h - T_c)}$. *Note: This requires knowing $\dot{m}_{total}$.* The make-up meter only measures evaporation + blowdown.
        2.  Let's use a simpler, direct feedback loop on **Fan Amperes ($I_{fan}$)** vs. **Cold Basin Temp ($T_c$)** for a given **Hot Basin Temp ($T_h$)**.
        3.  The failure is that $T_c$ is too high for the current ambient conditions. Since we can't measure ambient, we must assume that if we increase fan speed, we decrease $T_c$. The limit is when the approach no longer improves despite increased fan speed (inefficiency/fouling).
        4.  **Control Strategy:** Implement a **Feed-Forward Gain Schedule** on the fan control. Use $T_h$ and $T_c$ to determine the required fan power.
            *   Monitor $I_{fan}$.
            *   If $T_c$ is higher than the seasonal norm for a given $T_h$ (stored in a baseline curve from "last clean"), **increase the setpoint for fan current** (or run fans continuously instead of cycled).
            *   The mitigation is to **actively override the standard fan cycling schedule** to run at higher amperes when the calculated "Performance Gap" (difference between current $T_c$ and historical baseline $T_c$ at same $T_h$) exceeds a threshold.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Hot-Basin Thermometer ($T_h$):** Determines the thermal load entering the tower. A higher $T_h$ requires more fan power to maintain the same $T_c$.
*   **Cold-Basin Thermometer ($T_c$):** The primary feedback variable. The goal is to minimize $T_c$. The "widened approach" is detected as $T_c$ drifting higher than expected for the ambient conditions (approximated by the trend of $T_c$ relative to $T_h$ and season).
*   **Fan Motor Ammeters ($I_{fan}$):** Provides the input control variable. The mitigation adjusts the fan duty cycle/speed to draw higher current, thereby increasing air velocity and heat transfer, forcing $T_c$ down.
*   **Make-up Water Meter ($\dot{m}_{makeup}$):** Used as a health/fouling diagnostic. If the controller increases fan power ($I_{fan}$) but the make-up water rate (evaporation) does not increase proportionally to the increased air flow, it confirms fouling (scaling/filth) is the root cause, not just low fan speed. This allows the system to distinguish between "need more air" vs. "need cleaning." The mitigation algorithm will escalate fan power based on the *lack of response* in the make-up rate per ampere.

**Summary of Action:**
Establish a **Dynamic Fan Override Protocol** that monitors the ratio of **Make-up Water Flow** to **Fan Current** at a given **Hot Basin Temperature**. If this efficiency ratio drops below the baseline established after the last clean, automatically **increase the minimum fan current setpoint** (or eliminate fan-off periods) until the **Cold Basin Temperature** begins to decline, thereby narrowing the effective approach temperature despite the fouling. This uses only the four specified sensors: $T_h$ (load), $T_c$ (output), $I_{fan}$ (input/actuator), and $\dot{m}_{makeup}$ (performance feedback/fouling indicator).
