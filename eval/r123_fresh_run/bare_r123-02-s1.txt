**Mitigation Proposal: Closed-Loop Fan Speed Control with Real-Time Performance Indexing**

**Mechanism**
Implement a continuous, automatic control loop that modulates the cooling tower fan speed based on a real-time calculated "Performance Index" (PI) derived solely from the constraint-permitted existing sensors. The PI will quantify the divergence between the observed water temperature and the theoretical limit based on wet-bulb conditions. By continuously optimizing fan speed to keep the PI at its historical minimum (or within a strict band of it), the system compensates for seasonal fouling or heat exchanger degradation by increasing airflow, thereby restoring the approach temperature to its post-clean baseline without requiring new hardware.

**Implementation**
1.  **Wet-Bulb Estimation Module:** Since no new wet-bulb sensor is allowed, use the **hot-basin thermometer** (supply water temperature) and **ambient air temperature** (derived from the fan motor ammeters' correlation with airflow density, or assuming the control system already logs ambient dry-bulb via the BMS which is considered an "existing measurement" in most plant contexts; if strictly only the listed sensors are allowed, the PI is calculated relative to the *hot* water baseline). *Correction based on strict constraint:* We must rely *only* on hot/cold basin temps, fan ammeters, and makeup meter. The wet-bulb is not directly measured. Therefore, the mitigation shifts to **Optimal Control via Makeup Water and Flow Correlation**.
    *   *Revised Mechanism:* The widening approach is often caused by reduced effective heat transfer area (fouling) or airflow imbalance. The mitigation is to **automatically increase fan duty and verify circuit effectiveness via the make-up water meter**, using the **cold-basin temperature** as the primary setpoint.
    *   *Refined Implementation:*
        *   **Setpoint Tracking:** Define the target cold-basin temperature ($T_{cold,target}$) as a fixed offset above the *implied* wet-bulb. Since we lack a wet-bulb sensor, we use the **historical baseline**. The control system stores the "clean" relationship between $T_{cold}$ and Fan Amperage ($I_{fan}$).
        *   **Feedback Signal:** The error signal is the difference between the current **cold-basin thermometer** reading and the expected $T_{cold}$ for the current **fan motor ammeter** reading, based on the "clean" profile.
        *   **Control Action:** A PID controller adjusts the Variable Speed Drive (VSD) reference for the fans. If $T_{cold}$ is higher than the historical baseline for a given $I_{fan}$, the controller *increases* fan speed (increasing $I_{fan}$) until $T_{cold}$ matches the expected value for that new airflow.
        *   **Fouling Monitor (The Measure):** Use the **make-up water meter** to detect evaporation loss. As fouling increases, thermal efficiency drops, requiring higher fan speeds to achieve the same $T_{cold}$. Higher fan speeds often correlate with increased drift/evaporation loss. If the make-up water flow rises disproportionately relative to the increase in $I_{fan}$ (after normalizing for load), it signals significant degradation, prompting an alert for physical cleaning, but the *interim* mitigation is the sustained increase in fan duty.

**Measures Relied On**
1.  **Cold-Basin Thermometer:** Provides the real-time output ($T_{out}$) used as the primary process variable for the control loop.
2.  **Fan Motor Ammeters:** Provides the input ($u$) correlation. The amperage is a proxy for fan RPM and airflow mass. The control algorithm uses this to normalize the performance data (i.e., "For this amount of air, the water should be X degrees cooler. It is not. Therefore, we need more air.").
3.  **Make-up Water Meter:** Acts as a secondary validity check and drift monitor. An unexpected spike in make-up water usage, independent of a corresponding rise in fan amperage or hot water load, can indicate leakage or excessive drift due to high fan speeds, prompting a cap on fan speed to prevent water waste, thereby refining the optimization boundary.
4.  **Hot-Basin Thermometer:** Used to normalize the heat load. The control algorithm accounts for the temperature rise ($\Delta T = T_{hot} - T_{cold}$). If $T_{hot}$ varies, the required $T_{cold}$ setpoint shifts. The PI is calculated as $\frac{T_{hot} - T_{cold}}{T_{hot} - T_{wet-bulb}}$. Since $T_{wet-bulb}$ is unknown, we replace it with a *relative* index: $PI = \frac{T_{hot} - T_{cold}}{I_{fan}^k}$ where $k$ is a system-specific exponent determined during the last clean. The controller minimizes this PI by adjusting $I_{fan}$ via the VSD.

**Summary of Operation:**
The system continuously calculates a performance index based on the ratio of the approach temperature (approximated by $T_{hot} - T_{cold}$) to the fan amperage (proxy for airflow). As the tower fouls, this index degrades (increases). The controller automatically ramps up fan amperage (via VSD) to bring the index back within the "clean" baseline range, thereby artificially restoring the cold-water tracking capability until a physical cleaning is scheduled. The make-up water meter ensures that the increased airflow doesn't cause uncontrolled water loss, acting as a safety constraint on the mitigation.
