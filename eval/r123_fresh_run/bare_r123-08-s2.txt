    **Proposed Mitigation: Adaptive Condenser Setpoint Control with ΔT_cw Monitoring**
    
    **Mechanism:**
    Implement a control strategy that lowers the condenser water inlet temperature setpoint (by increasing cooling tower fan speed and valve opening, if available, or simply prioritizing the chiller's cooling mode) and uses the calculated **Condenser Water Temperature Rise (ΔT_cw)** to trigger an alarm for potential flow restriction or fouling.
    
    **Implementation:**
    1.  **Calculate ΔT_cw:** Use the existing sensors to compute `DeltaT_cw = T_cond_out - T_cond_in`.
    2.  **Alarm on High ΔT_cw:** Set a high-limit alarm on `DeltaT_cw`. A high ΔT_cw indicates low condenser water flow (e.g., clogged strainer, low pump speed). This is a critical mitigation step because low flow causes the "wide approach" and high head pressure.
    3.  **Alarm on High T_cond_out:** Set a high-limit alarm on `T_cond_out` relative to a baseline. If `DeltaT_cw` is normal but `T_cond_out` is high, it indicates fouling or insufficient heat rejection capacity (e.g., cooling tower not working, high ambient).
    4.  **Optimization Loop:** If the building control system allows, adjust the condenser water inlet temperature setpoint to the minimum required to maintain a safe condenser water differential, thereby maximizing chiller efficiency (COP). Lowering T_cond_in reduces head pressure and compressor kW/ton.

    1.  **Existing Sensors:** T_cond_in, T_cond_out, T_evap_in, T_evap_out, Compressor kW.
    2.  **Constraint Compliance:** No new instrumentation. The logic is pure computation from existing points.
    3.  **Operational Action:** The mitigation is *diagnostic and protective*. It uses the existing data to pinpoint the root cause (Flow vs. Fouling) and allows operators to intervene (clean strainers, adjust cooling towers) to recover the "wide approach" and reduce kW/ton.
