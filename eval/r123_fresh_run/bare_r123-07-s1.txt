**Mitigation Proposal**

**Mechanism:**
Implement a **Pressure-Bounded Pulse-Cleaning Algorithm** that dynamically adjusts the compressed-air valve dwell time and pulse frequency based on the real-time differential pressure (DP) rise rate, using the clean-air plume opacity meter to validate cleaning efficacy and prevent over-cleaning that contributes to air consumption.

The core mechanism shifts from a fixed-time periodic cleaning to a **DP-triggered, opacity-verified cleaning mode**. The climbing DP after each cycle indicates that the filter media is re-encrusted before the next scheduled clean (due to excessive dust cake buildup or "blinding") while the rising air consumption suggests the valves are opening for too long or the system pressure is being compensated by a failing regulator, wasting air. The algorithm restricts air usage by only cleaning when DP exceeds a threshold and stopping immediately when opacity spikes drop (indicating clean fabric), thereby reducing total air volume per clean and preventing the cycle-by-cycle DP accumulation by ensuring each clean is *effective* rather than merely *frequent*.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Modify the PLC/Controller Logic:** Reprogram the existing cleaning timer to monitor the **baghouse DP taps** continuously. Establish a dynamic "Clean-Initiate" threshold (e.g., DP > 4.0 in. w.c.) and a "Clean-Stop" threshold (e.g., DP drop of 10% from peak or opacity meter reads >95% transmission).
2.  **Opacity-Gated Termination:** Integrate the **clean-air plume opacity meter** as the primary stop criterion. The pulse sequence stops as soon as the opacity meter indicates the exhaust plume has cleared (low opacity). This prevents the "blind" extended pulsing that wastes compressed air and can re-blind the filter with loose dust.
3.  **Regulator Diagnostics Loop:** Use the **compressed-air pressure regulator gauge** signal (if 4-20mA/Analog input available) or periodic manual logging to establish a baseline. If the DP rise rate post-clean is steep *and* the opacity meter shows persistent high opacity during initial pulses, the controller flags a "Media Failure" or "Valve Leakage" condition, reducing pulse frequency to a maintenance crawl (max 1 pulse/10 min) to isolate the issue without wasting air.

**Measures It Relies On:**
*   **Baghouse DP Taps:** Used to trigger the start of the cleaning sequence (replacing time-based timers) and to monitor the rate of DP recovery post-pulse. A slow DP drop indicates the fabric is "blinded" or the pulse is ineffective, prompting the opacity check.
*   **Clean-Air Plume Opacity Meter:** Used as the **terminating condition** for the cleaning sequence. High opacity (low transmission) signifies that dust is still being discharged or the fabric is saturated; low opacity confirms the fabric is permeable and clean. This directly reduces air consumption by cutting off pulses the moment the fabric is clean, rather than running a fixed duration.
*   **Compressed-Air Pressure Regulator Gauge:** Used to verify that the supply pressure remains stable during cleaning. If the gauge shows a significant pressure drop during pulse events while the DP does not recover, it indicates an under-sized air supply or a leaking manifold, prompting the operator to adjust the regulator or reduce pulse frequency to compensate, thereby conserving total air consumption.
