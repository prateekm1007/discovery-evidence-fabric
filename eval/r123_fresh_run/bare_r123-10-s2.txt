**Mitigation Proposal: Adaptive Reflux-Pressure Ratio Control Loop**

**Mechanism:**
The flattening of the temperature profile and increased reflux demand indicate that the column is operating with insufficient theoretical stages due to either reduced vapor traffic (low boilup) or inefficient mass transfer, likely caused by a mismatch between the current reflux flow and the column’s internal pressure drop. The core mechanism is to decouple the reflux ratio from a fixed setpoint and instead modulate it dynamically based on the real-time ratio of **Reflux Flow** to **Column Differential Pressure**. This ratio serves as a proxy for the liquid-to-vapor mass balance efficiency. By tightening this specific ratio, the control system ensures that for every unit of vapor traffic (indicated by ΔP), the liquid load (reflux) is optimized to maintain the steepest possible temperature gradient, thereby restoring separation sharpness without requiring new sensors.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Calculate the Process Variable (PV):** Create a new derived tag in the DCS/PLC: `PV_RefluxPressure = (Reflux Flow Meter Reading) / (Column ΔP Transmitter Reading)`.
2.  **Define Operating Envelope:** Analyze historical data from when the column was performing optimally to establish the "Target Range" for `PV_RefluxPressure`. This range represents the optimal liquid-to-vapor load factor.
3.  **Modify Control Logic:**
    *   Switch the Reflux Flow Control Valve (FCV) from a standard setpoint control to a **Ratio Control** mode.
    *   The Reflux Flow Controller (FIC) receives its setpoint not from a constant value, but from the output of a Pressure-Referenced Ratio Controller.
    *   Alternatively, implement a cascade loop where the primary controller manages the `PV_RefluxPressure` ratio, and the secondary loop adjusts the Reflux FCV to maintain that ratio against disturbances in vapor generation (boilup).
4.  **Operator Interface:** Display the calculated `PV_RefluxPressure` ratio on the HMI. Alert the operator if the ratio drifts outside the historically validated optimal band, prompting a check on the reboiler or feed conditions if the ratio control alone cannot recover the profile.

**Measures Relied Upon (Existing Sensors Only):**
*   **Reflux Flow Meter:** Provides the absolute liquid load returning to the column.
*   **Column Differential Pressure Transmitter (ΔP):** Serves as a reliable, existing proxy for total vapor traffic and hydrodynamic load within the column. A stable ΔP with rising reflux indicates poor efficiency; a changing ΔP allows the ratio to self-correct for load variations.
*   **Tray Temperature Multipoints:** Used offline for validation only. While not part of the real-time control loop to avoid instability from noisy temperature lags, the operator uses these measurements to verify that the temperature profile has sharpened (increased slope) after the new ratio control is implemented. The constraint prohibits *adding* sensors, but existing ones can be used for monitoring/verification. The control action itself relies solely on the flow and ΔP signals.

**Why This Works Under Constraints:**
This mitigation uses no new hardware. It exploits the physical relationship that column efficiency is highly sensitive to the balance between liquid load (reflux) and vapor load (pressure drop). By dynamically matching these two existing measurements, the system automatically corrects for the "flattening" caused by inefficient liquid distribution or vapor entrainment, restoring the temperature gradient needed for top purity without needing new temperature-based feedback loops that might be slow or laggy.
