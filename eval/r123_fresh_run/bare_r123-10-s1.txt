**Mitigation Proposal: Dynamic Reflux Ratio Optimization via Tray Temperature Gradient Mapping**

**Mechanism**
The core issue is that the flattened temperature profile indicates a loss of stage efficiency (likely due to flooding, weeping, or feed composition drift) while the high reflux demand suggests the control system is over-compensating with excessive vapor/liquid traffic to maintain purity. The mitigation relies on **shifting from a fixed or conservatively high reflux ratio to a dynamically optimized reflux ratio** derived from the *gradient* of the existing tray temperature multipoints. By calculating the slope of the temperature change across the section of trays where separation is most critical (the stripping and rectifying sections near the feed point), the system can infer the effective number of theoretical stages. A steeper gradient implies better separation efficiency, allowing for a *lower* reflux ratio to be maintained while holding top purity. Conversely, if the gradient flattens, the system knows to increase reflux *just enough* to restore the gradient slope, rather than blindly maximizing reflux. This decouples purity control from energy-wasting over-refluxing.

**Implementation**
1.  **Data Acquisition**: Utilize the **tray temperature multipoints** to calculate the temperature difference (ΔT) across a defined set of consecutive trays (e.g., 3–5 trays below the feed stage and 3–5 trays above). Calculate the local temperature gradient (slope) for both the rectifying and stripping sections.
2.  **Process Inference**: Use the **reflux flow meter** and **column differential pressure (DP) transmitter** to normalize the gradient. High DP may indicate foaming or hydraulic issues that artificially flatten the temperature profile regardless of reflux. The algorithm must flag periods where DP is outside the normal operating envelope, suspending gradient-based optimization and reverting to a safe, higher reflux setpoint.
3.  **Control Logic**: Implement a soft-sensor or advanced controller logic (within existing DCS/PLC capabilities) that maps the calculated *effective separation efficiency* (derived from temperature gradients) to an *optimal reflux ratio* setpoint. This optimal setpoint is calculated to maintain the required top product purity (monitored via indirect inference from top tray temperatures, as no new sensors are allowed) with the minimum necessary reflux.
4.  **Feedback Loop**: The reflux flow meter provides the actual flow. The controller adjusts the reflux valve (assuming existing actuator) to track the dynamically calculated optimal reflux setpoint, not a static high value. The top tray temperature (from the multipoints) serves as a slow-acting corrective bias: if top temperature trends up, slightly increase reflux; if it trends down, decrease reflux.

**Measures Relied Upon**
*   **Tray Temperature Multipoints**: Provide the spatial data necessary to compute temperature gradients, which are a proxy for local mass transfer efficiency and stage effectiveness. The trend in these gradients directly reveals whether the flattening is due to hydraulic issues (correlated with DP) or composition/efficiency loss.
*   **Column Differential Pressure Transmitter**: Acts as the critical discriminator between "flattening due to loss of vapor-liquid contact" (e.g., low liquid load) vs. "flattening due to hydraulic instability/flooding" (high DP). It prevents the optimization algorithm from pushing reflux into an unstable hydraulic regime.
*   **Reflux Flow Meter**: Provides the necessary feedback for precise adjustment of the reflux stream. It allows the system to quantify the energy input and close the loop on the dynamic setpoint, ensuring that the reduction in reflux does not compromise purity, as verified by the top tray temperature trends from the multipoints.

**Why This Works Under the Constraint**
This mitigation does not require new sensors. It repurposes the existing temperature array from a mere diagnostic tool into a real-time process efficiency estimator. By focusing on the *shape* of the temperature profile (gradient) rather than just absolute temperatures, it directly addresses the "flattening" symptom. The DP transmitter guards against unsafe operation, and the reflux meter enables the energy-saving action of reducing unnecessary reflux, thereby mitigating the "grown reflux demand" while maintaining separation quality through smarter, not just stronger, control.
