The central hypothesis is that optimal predictive power for molecular properties and reaction outcomes is achieved not by relying on a single type of descriptor, but by synergistically combining complementary feature spaces: graph-based deep learning representations that capture local topological context, and physics-informed descriptors that encode specific chemical semantics.

Molecular fingerprints and standard structural descriptors fail because they treat molecules as static bit strings or isolated graphs, ignoring the electronic and quantum mechanical context that dictates reactivity and physical behavior. The mechanism behind improved prediction lies in "feature complementarity": deep learning embeddings (such as those from Graph Neural Networks) excel at capturing complex, non-linear structural patterns but often lack inductive bias for specific chemical phenomena (e.g., exact bond orders or charge distributions). Conversely, hand-crafted descriptors (like RDKit descriptors or quantum chemical properties) provide precise, interpretable signals for specific mechanisms but are sparse and may not generalize across diverse chemical spaces.

To exploit this mechanism, practitioners should construct a heterogeneous feature set that concatenates these distinct types of information. Specifically, one should employ an ensemble approach where the input to the machine learning model includes:
1.  **Learned Embeddings:** Generated via Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that process the molecular graph to create dense vectors capturing global and local structural context.
2.  **Semantic Descriptors:** A curated set of traditional molecular descriptors (e.g., topological indices, electronic descriptors like HOMO/LUMO gaps, and steric properties) that explicitly encode chemical knowledge.
3.  **Contextual Features:** For reaction outcomes, include descriptors for the reactants, catalysts, and solvent environment, as these external factors are critical determinants of selectivity and rate but are often ignored in single-molecule property prediction.

This hybrid feature set allows the model to use learned embeddings for broad pattern recognition and semantic descriptors to ground the predictions in chemical reality, reducing overfitting and improving generalization to out-of-distribution molecules. The workflow should be user-friendly by automating the generation of both embedding and descriptor features through a unified pipeline, followed by automated feature selection (such as mutual information or L1-regularization) to prune redundant descriptors and retain only the most predictive signals.
