I hypothesize that the mechanism for generating a diverse library of heterocyclic scaffolds with optimized antimicrobial potency involves coupling asymmetric multicomponent reactions (MCRs) catalyzed by cinchonine derivatives with in silico-driven selection, rather than relying solely on empirical screening. Specifically, I posit that cinchonine-derived organocatalysts can efficiently drive the formation of chiral heterocyclic cores—such as pyridines or quinoxalines—from simple aldehydes, amines, and carbonyl partners in a one-pot fashion, thereby rapidly expanding structural diversity with defined stereochemistry. To exploit this mechanism, one must design a modular MCR protocol where the reactants are varied systematically to generate a combinatorial library of chiral heterocycles. Subsequently, this library should be subjected to computational filtering using molecular docking against specific bacterial or fungal targets (e.g., dihydrofolate reductase) and MM-GBSA scoring to predict binding affinity and complex stability. By integrating in silico ADMET profiling early in the workflow, one can prioritize scaffolds that are not only potent inhibitors but also possess favorable drug-like properties and structural stability, effectively bridging synthetic diversity with pharmacokinetic viability before biological testing.
