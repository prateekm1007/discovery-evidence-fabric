To selectively functionalize electron-deficient, non-aromatic heterocycles and alkenes with aryl groups without relying on traditional aryl halide cross-coupling precursors, the mechanism must pivot from the standard Catellani framework, which is fundamentally dependent on aryl-Pd intermediates. Instead, the hypothesis centers on a cooperative catalytic cycle where palladium acts not just as a coupling partner but as a Lewis acid and migratory insertion mediator, working in concert with a secondary catalyst or co-catalyst (such as a silylating agent or a distinct electrophilic activator).

The proposed mechanistic pathway involves the initial activation of the electron-deficient alkene or heterocyclic substrate by the palladium center through oxidative addition or, more likely in the absence of metal-halide precursors, via a Lewis acid-promoted alkene activation. In this scenario, an alternative aryl source, such as aryl boronic acids, aryl stannanes, or even aryl halides that do not undergo standard oxidative addition but instead participate in transmetallation or nucleophilic attack pathways, is introduced. The key distinction is that the palladium species facilitates the migration of the aryl group onto the substrate in a manner analogous to Heck or Suzuki reactions, but directed by a specific geometric constraint provided by a co-catalyst (like norbornene or a chiral ligand) that stabilizes the transient pallacycle or open-shell intermediate. 

To exploit this mechanism for selective functionalization, the strategy should leverage the differential reactivity of electron-poor alkenes. Since these substrates are highly susceptible to nucleophilic attack, the aryl source should be generated in a nucleophilic form (e.g., via transmetallation to a palladium-aryl species that acts as a nucleophile in a formal C-H arylation or insertion reaction). The cooperativity arises from the second catalyst component, which might serve to activate the C-H bond of the aryl precursor or to stabilize the transition state for the migratory insertion, thereby lowering the activation energy for the reaction with the sluggish, non-aromatic substrate. This allows for the bypass of the traditional Catellani requirement for a pre-activated aryl halide, instead using a cooperative dual-catalyst system that couples an aryl nucleophile with the electrophilic character of the electron-deficient alkene/heterocycle, enabling the installation of aryl groups on complex motifs like glycals and quinolones with high regio- and chemo-selectivity.
