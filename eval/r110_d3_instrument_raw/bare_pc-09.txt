The proposed mechanism for selectively forming carbon-carbon bonds between tertiary alkyl fragments and alkenes or arenes utilizes a synergistic dual-catalytic system involving nickel and photoredox chemistry. Under mild light irradiation, a photoredox catalyst mediates single-electron transfer processes to generate a tertiary alkyl radical from an unactivated alkyl electrophile, such as a tertiary alkyl halide. This radical is highly unstable under thermal conditions but can be stabilized transiently by interacting with the nickel catalyst, which is capable of accessing multiple redox states. The nickel center captures the alkyl radical to form an organonickel intermediate. This intermediate then undergoes cross-coupling with the electron-deficient alkene or activated arene partner. The selectivity is exploited by carefully tuning the redox potentials of the photoredox cycle and the ligand environment of the nickel to ensure that the radical generation is selective for the tertiary fragment, while the subsequent nickel-mediated reductive elimination efficiently forms the new C-C bond without undergoing competing side reactions. This approach bypasses the limitations of traditional cross-coupling methods that struggle with sp3 hybridized carbons, allowing for the access to complex molecular architectures.
