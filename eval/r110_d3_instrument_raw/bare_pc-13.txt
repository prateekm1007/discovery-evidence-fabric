The primary mechanism governing the structural formation and OER performance of these multi-cation nickel spinels is that precursor solution pH dictates the nucleation density and particle size distribution of the amorphous hydroxide intermediates, which subsequently determines the final porosity and specific surface area, while calcination temperature and duration control the degree of cation ordering within the spinel lattice and the extent of sintering. Specifically, a lower pH promotes rapid precipitation of smaller nuclei, leading to a highly porous network with enhanced mass transport, whereas a higher pH favors larger crystallites with reduced active site density. Regarding thermal processing, increasing the calcination temperature initially improves the crystallinity and cation distribution on tetrahedral and octahedral sites, optimizing the electronic structure for OER, but excessive temperatures or prolonged durations cause severe sintering and grain growth, which reduces the specific surface area and blocks active sites, thereby degrading catalytic efficiency. To exploit this, one should optimize the precursor pH to target a specific particle size that balances high surface area with sufficient structural integrity, and then apply a calcination schedule that achieves the optimal cation ordering temperature without inducing significant grain coarsening, thereby maximizing the number of accessible, electronically favorable multi-cation active sites.
