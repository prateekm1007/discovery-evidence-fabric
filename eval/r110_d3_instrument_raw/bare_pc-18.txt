The mechanism for stabilizing a three-dimensional anionic framework in a metal selenide system relies on leveraging the bartonite structural archetype, which naturally resolves the inherent instability of isolated low-dimensional chains by interlinking them into a robust 3D network through shared corner tetrahedra. To exploit this, one must synthesize mixed-metal solid solutions where larger cations substitute for iron at specific sites within the framework. This substitution tunes the lattice parameters and charge balance, effectively preventing the collapse of the selenide network back into 1D or 2D phases. The key exploitation strategy involves precise compositional control to ensure that the anionic [Se] framework remains fully coordinated and sterically supported by the mixed-metal cationic lattice, thereby overcoming the entropic and enthalpic preferences for lower-dimensional structures typically observed in simple iron selenides.
