The hypothesis is that the covalent functionalization of single-walled carbon nanotubes with aryldiazonium salts can be exploited to create quantum emitters through a mild, ambient-temperature reaction mechanism that proceeds without external physical stimuli. By leveraging the inherent reactivity of aryldiazonium ions toward the nanotube sidewall, we can form stable carbon-carbon bonds that introduce sp3 defects, which act as single-photon emitters. This process is scalable and energy-efficient because it occurs spontaneously under mild conditions, avoiding the need for laser irradiation or electron beams. Crucially, the reaction is self-reporting; the progress of the covalent attachment can be monitored in real-time via spectral changes in the nanotube's fluorescence or absorption features. This allows for precise quality control and ensures high throughput by halting the reaction exactly when the desired density of quantum defects is achieved, thus eliminating hazardous reagents and minimizing waste while maintaining high purity and reproducibility.
