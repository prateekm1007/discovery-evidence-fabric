While cellulase is primarily known for hydrolyzing cellulosic biomass, in the synthesis of metal nanoparticles, it acts not just as a catalyst for precursor breakdown but also as a mild, green reducing and stabilizing agent. The mechanism involves the enzyme's ability to interact with the silver ions, facilitating their reduction into elemental silver nuclei. By adding cellulase to the *Aspergillus niger* extract, you exploit the enzymatic specificity to control the nucleation rate. A controlled, slower reduction rate driven by the enzyme prevents uncontrolled rapid aggregation of silver atoms, which is the primary cause of larger particle sizes and lower yields in traditional chemical methods. Therefore, the exploitation of this mechanism involves optimizing the concentration of cellulase to ensure it acts as the dominant reducing agent, thereby promoting the formation of numerous, small nucleation sites rather than few, large ones. This leads to a final product with a smaller average particle size, narrower size distribution, and higher yield due to improved stability and reduced agglomeration.
