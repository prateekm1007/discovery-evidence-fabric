**Verdict:** The draft violates the stated constraint and relies on unavailable resources.

**Reasoning:**
1.  **Constraint Violation:** The constraint explicitly states, "No new instrumentation may be added." The draft proposes to "Install an online deposit-thickness sensor." This is a direct violation of the constraint, as installing a new sensor constitutes adding new instrumentation.
2.  **Reliance on Unavailable Resources:** The constraint limits the available measurements to "existing sensors and existing measurements: the brine inlet/outlet thermocouples and the flow meter already installed." An "online deposit-thickness sensor" is not listed among the available measurements and is therefore unavailable.
3.  **Physical/Logical Issues:** While the physical direction (triggering backflush when fouling increases) is not inverted, the proposal fails because it cannot be implemented within the defined system boundaries.

A valid mitigation would need to infer fouling status (and thus trigger backflush) using only the existing brine inlet/outlet temperatures and flow rate (e.g., by calculating the heat transfer coefficient or observing the drift in outlet temperature for a given load), rather than installing a dedicated thickness sensor.
