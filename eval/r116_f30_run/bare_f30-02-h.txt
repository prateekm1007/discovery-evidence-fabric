**Verdict:** The draft proposal **misprices the duty cycle** and **inverts the physical direction** of the mitigation effect relative to the stated cost constraint. Therefore, it is **not acceptable**.

**Reasoning:**

1.  **Mispricing of Duty Cycle (Critical Error):**
    *   The draft proposes changing from *continuous* dosing to a *periodic* schedule (2 minutes ON every 30 minutes).
    *   Let's calculate the new duty cycle: $2 \text{ min} / 30 \text{ min} = 1/6 \approx 16.7\%$.
    *   Continuous dosing implies a 100% duty cycle.
    *   Therefore, the new schedule uses approximately **83.3% less** defoamer than continuous dosing.
    *   The draft claims this results in "**+18%** defoamer spend." This is a fundamental mathematical and logical error. A reduction in duty cycle from 100% to ~17% cannot result in an *increase* in spend. It should result in a significant *decrease* in spend (specifically, a ~83% decrease relative to continuous dosing, or a cost statement that acknowledges the reduction).
    *   Even if the intent was to compare the new pulse dose concentration to the *previous* periodic dose (which isn't stated), claiming a +18% cost increase for a schedule that drastically reduces total volume is incorrect unless the flow rate of the pump during the 2-minute pulse is increased by a factor of 1.18 * 18 (i.e., ~21.24x the continuous rate), which is not stated and would be physically unreasonable for a "pulse" dosing strategy aimed at cost control. The standard interpretation is that total consumption scales with duty cycle at constant flow, so the cost must decrease.

2.  **Inversion of Physical/Operational Direction (Constraint Violation):**
    *   The constraint states: *"any change to its duty schedule must carry an operational cost statement (defoamer is priced per litre)."*
    *   The draft *does* carry a cost statement. However, the direction of the cost impact is inverted. By claiming a cost *increase* (+18%) for a duty cycle *reduction*, the proposal fails to correctly represent the operational consequence.
    *   More critically, in the context of "high organic load" causing foaming, moving from continuous to a low-duty-cycle pulse (16.7%) is likely to be **ineffective** at controlling the foam, leading to *more* carryover, not less. If the goal is mitigation, a reduction in dosing frequency might be wrong direction physically for the failure mode (unless the foam is intermittent). However, the primary flaw in the *review criteria* provided is the **mispricing**. The proposal misstates the cost impact of its own proposed schedule.

3.  **Reliance on Unavailable Items:**
    *   No explicit reliance on unavailable hardware or resources is stated, only a change in pump schedule and a cost claim.

4.  **Constraint Violation Summary:**
    *   The constraint requires an accurate operational cost statement. The statement provided is factually incorrect regarding the direction of cost impact (claiming an increase when a decrease is expected from reduced duty cycle). This constitutes a failure to provide a valid cost statement as required.

**Conclusion:**
The draft is rejected because it **misprices the duty cycle**. Reducing the duty cycle from continuous (100%) to ~16.7% will reduce defoamer consumption and associated costs, not increase them by 18%. The proposal must be revised to correctly calculate the cost impact (likely a significant reduction) and justify why such a reduction in dosing is sufficient to mitigate foaming under high organic load conditions.
