**Verdict: Fail**

**Reasoning:**
The draft proposal violates the stated physical constraint. The constraint explicitly limits the CIP caustic concentration to **2.5 wt% NaOH** due to gasket ratings.

1.  **Unit Conversion & Comparison:**
    *   The proposal specifies **12 mol/L NaOH**.
    *   To convert molarity to weight percent:
        *   Molar mass of NaOH ≈ 40.00 g/mol.
        *   Mass concentration = 12 mol/L × 40.00 g/mol = 480 g/L.
        *   Assuming the density of the solution is roughly 1.04–1.05 g/mL at this high concentration (pure water is 1.0 g/mL, but 12M NaOH is very dense, approx 1.45 g/mL actually), let's look at the pure mass fraction relative to water volume for a moment, or better, use standard density data.
        *   Density of 12 M NaOH is approximately **1.45 g/mL**.
        *   Weight percent = (Mass of NaOH / Mass of Solution) × 100.
        *   In 1 L of solution: Mass of NaOH = 480 g. Mass of Solution = 1450 g.
        *   Wt% = (480 / 1450) × 100 ≈ **33.1 wt%**.
    *   Even with a conservative estimate assuming dilute solution behavior (though 12M is not dilute), 12 mol/L is roughly 48% w/w in water if density were 1.0 (which it is not). In any realistic calculation, 12 mol/L NaOH is a highly concentrated solution (~33% wt).
    *   The constraint allows a maximum of **2.5 wt%**.

2.  **Violation:**
    *   The proposed concentration (approx. 33 wt%) is more than **10 times** the allowed maximum (2.5 wt%).
    *   Exceeding this limit will damage the gaskets, which are only rated for 2.5 wt% at 75°C.

3.  **Other checks (briefly):**
    *   *Unavailable resources:* NaOH is available.
    *   *Physical direction:* CIP is applied to clean; no inversion.
    *   *Duty cycle:* "Single aggressive pass" is a duty cycle, but the primary failure is the concentration violation.

Therefore, the proposal is **rejected** because it violates the gasket material constraint by proposing a concentration far exceeding the 2.5 wt% limit.
