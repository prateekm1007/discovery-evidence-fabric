**Mitigation Proposal: Implement Dynamic Temperature-Compensated Equalization Scheduling with Lowered Hysteresis**

**Mechanism:**
The failure is driven by thermal accumulation during standard equalization cycles, which raises cell temperature above ambient, accelerating electrolyte dry-out (gassing) via the Arrhenius law. To mitigate this, the mitigation shifts from a fixed-time/fixed-voltage equalization schedule to a **temperature-constrained, time-scaled equalization strategy**. Specifically, the inverter’s equalization routine is modified to dynamically lower the equalization voltage and shorten the duration whenever the battery room temperature exceeds a baseline (e.g., >22°C). This reduces the overpotential applied to the cells, thereby minimizing the gassing rate and the associated heat generation, while still providing enough energy to remove sulfation.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Sensors & Control Loop:** Install a high-accuracy temperature sensor (±0.1°C) at the mid-stack location of the hottest VRLA string. Feed this into the UPS/inverter controller’s BMS logic.
2.  **Voltage Scaling Algorithm:** Define an equalization voltage offset that is inversely proportional to ambient temperature deviation. For example: $V_{eq} = V_{std} - k \cdot (T_{amb} - T_{base})$, where $T_{base} = 20°C$, $T_{amb}$ is the current room temperature, and $k$ is a coefficient derived from the manufacturer’s temperature compensation data (typically ~3–5 mV/°C per cell for float, but adjusted for equalization depth).
3.  **Time Scaling:** Reduce the equalization duration proportionally. If the voltage is lowered by ΔV, reduce the charge time by a factor that maintains equivalent amp-hour delivery (Time_new = Time_std * (V_std / V_new)^n), or simply cap the maximum equalization duration at high temperatures to prevent thermal runaway of the process.
4.  **Setpoint Adjustment:** Lower the battery room air conditioning setpoint to **18°C** (the lower limit of the constraint). This creates a larger thermal margin below the 26°C upper limit, ensuring that even if cooling fails briefly, the temperature stays in the optimal range for VRLA life.

**Measures Relied On:**
1.  **Arrhenius Kinetics Reduction:** By lowering the room setpoint to 18°C and reducing the equalization voltage (which reduces ohmic and gassing heat), the cell temperature during equalization is kept significantly lower. Since the dry-out rate follows $k \propto e^{-E_a/RT}$, even a 5°C reduction in peak cell temperature can halve the aging rate constant for the dry-out leg ($E_a \approx 80$ kJ/mol).
2.  **Thermal Margin Management:** Utilizing the full 18–26°C range by setting the lower bound (18°C) ensures that the “sustained high ambient” condition is never met. The thermal mass of the room and the shorter, cooler equalization cycles prevent the cumulative thermal stress that causes capacity fade between equalizations.
3.  **Gassing Rate Control:** Lowering the equalization voltage directly reduces the hydrogen and oxygen recombination/gassing rate, which is the primary mechanism for electrolyte loss in VRLA batteries. This preserves the electrolyte concentration and prevents the dry-out acceleration observed in the failure description.
