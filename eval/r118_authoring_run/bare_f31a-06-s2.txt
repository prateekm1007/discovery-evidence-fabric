**Mitigation Proposal: Optimize CIP Protocol with Maximally Permissible Caustic Concentration and Pre-Soak**

**Mechanism**
The failure mode of "blistering" scale caused by hard water, combined with a caustic Clean-in-Place (CIP) that is "losing bite," indicates that the current alkaline concentration and thermal conditions are insufficient to hydrolyze the organic matrix and soften the mineral scale before the acid wash. By raising the caustic concentration to the maximum limit defined by the gasket rating, we maximize the saponification of fats/proteins and the swelling/detachment of the inorganic scale matrix. This increases the "bite" (surface tension and chemical reactivity) of the CIP, preventing the scale from blisters and facilitating its removal during the subsequent acid descaling step.

**Implementation**
1.  **Increase Caustic Concentration:** Adjust the CIP alkali dosage from the current level to **2.5 wt% NaOH**. This is the upper limit specified by the gasket rating, ensuring maximum chemical efficacy without exceeding material compatibility limits.
2.  **Thermal Optimization:** Maintain the CIP temperature at **75°C**. This is the highest temperature at which the 2.5 wt% NaOH solution is safe for the gaskets. Operating at this temperature maximizes the reaction kinetics for alkaline hydrolysis and scale loosening.
3.  **Extended Pre-Soak Cycle:** Implement a static soak phase of at least **20–30 minutes** at 75°C with 2.5 wt% NaOH before the recirculation rinse. This allows the high-concentration caustic to penetrate the scale blisters fully, hydrating the mineral deposits and breaking the bond with the plate surface.
4.  **Verification:** After the next acid descaling cycle, inspect the pasteurizer plates for residual blisters. Monitor conductivity or pH of the caustic drain to ensure the concentration remains at 2.5 wt% throughout the cycle, indicating no dilution or failure of the chemical supply system.

**Measures Relied Upon**
*   **Chemical Kinetics:** Higher concentration (2.5 wt%) and temperature (75°C) exponentially increase the rate of alkaline scale loosening and organic matter hydrolysis compared to lower-concentration baselines.
*   **Material Compatibility Limits:** The proposal strictly adheres to the gasket rating (2.5 wt% NaOH @ 75°C), utilizing the full operational envelope of the system without risking mechanical failure (gasket degradation/blowout).
*   **Synergistic CIP Sequence:** The enhanced caustic step prepares the surface for the acid step by removing organic films and hydrating the scale, making the subsequent acid descaling more effective and reducing the frequency of full mechanical cleaning or emergency acid shocks.
