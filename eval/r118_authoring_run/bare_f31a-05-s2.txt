**Mitigation Proposal: Controlled Manual Greasing Protocol with "Purge-First" Verification**

**Mechanism:**
The failure mode (outer race spalling) is caused by grease washover, which indicates that the bearing is being over-lubricated. Excess grease increases internal friction and temperature, causing the grease to soften, lose its structural film strength, and eventually be squeezed out or break down, leading to starvation and spalling. Since automatic systems and grease changes are prohibited, the mitigation relies on strictly controlling the *quantity* and *timing* of the existing manual greasing route to ensure the bearing is filled to capacity but never over-filled.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Establish a "Grease Full" Baseline:** Before the next scheduled service, stop all greasing. Allow the bearing to run at full temperature for one full grease life cycle (as defined by the OEM, typically 1–3 months depending on speed and load). During this period, visually inspect the grease purge points (seals or relief ports) to identify the exact point at which clean grease begins to emerge from the bearing. Mark this point in time or mark the grease gun stroke count (if a calibrated manual gun is used) as the "Fill Point."
2.  **Institute a "Purge-First" Rule:** Mandate that no new grease is added until the operator confirms the previous fill has been properly purged. This involves:
    *   Allowing the bearing to operate until grease emerges from the relief/seal point, confirming the old grease and any excess have been expelled.
    *   Wiping away the purge grease.
    *   Only then injecting the calculated "Fill Volume" (determined in Step 1) using the existing manual route.
3.  **Implement Quantitative Grease Injection:** If the current manual process is time-based (e.g., "grease for 2 minutes"), convert it to a volume/stroke-based process. Use the calibrated grease gun to deliver a specific number of strokes (e.g., 5–8 strokes) into the fitting. Record this number on the maintenance log as the standard "Fill Dose."
4.  **Extend Intervals, Not Reduce Amount:** Do not reduce the amount of grease per session; instead, strictly enforce the interval between sessions. Ensure the interval is long enough for the bearing to operate at full temperature and purge completely. If washover persists, extend the interval by 10–20% each cycle until no washover occurs, then lock that interval.

**Measures Relied Upon:**
*   **OEM Grease Spec (Fixed):** The grease itself is unchanged, so its temperature-softening characteristics remain constant. The mitigation works because it respects the fixed grease’s physical limits by preventing the thermal overload caused by excess volume.
*   **Bearing Thermal Equilibrium:** Relies on the fact that bearings generate heat during operation. A properly filled bearing will reach a stable temperature where the grease viscosity is optimal. Over-greased bearings fail to reach this stable state due to churning; this protocol ensures the bearing operates in its designed thermal range.
*   **Manual Process Discipline:** Relies on operator compliance with the new "Purge-First" and "Stroke-Count" rules. No hardware changes are made; only the procedural control of the existing manual route is tightened.
*   **Visual Purge Indicator:** Relies on the physical presence of grease at the relief point as the definitive signal that the bearing is full and old grease has been expelled, making it safe to add new grease.

**Outcome:**
By eliminating the root cause (over-pressurization and thermal degradation from excess grease) through procedural control of quantity and timing, the grease film remains intact, the outer race no longer spalls, and the bearing life is extended indefinitely within the fixed constraints.
