**Mitigation Proposal: Implement a Temperature-Compensated Absorption Voltage Control Loop to Maintain the Electrolyte at its Optimal Saturation Point**

**Mechanism**
The core mechanism is to exploit the negative temperature coefficient of the required absorption voltage for VRLA batteries. As ambient temperature rises, the required voltage to achieve the target charge efficiency (and thus maintain electrolyte hydration through the AGM separator) decreases. Standard, fixed-voltage charging leads to overcharge at high temperatures, accelerating water electrolysis and gas recombination losses (dry-out). Conversely, undercharge at low temperatures leads to sulfation. By dynamically lowering the absorption voltage setpoint as ambient temperature increases, the system prevents excessive water splitting. This keeps the electrolyte within the optimal saturation window of the AGM mats, mitigating the Arrhenius-driven dry-out rate without relying solely on the room’s thermal mass.

**Implementation**
1.  **Sensor Integration:** Install a digital temperature sensor (PT100 or DS18B20) directly on the string’s hottest cell (typically the middle cell) rather than relying on room ambient sensors alone.
2.  **Battery Management System (BMS) Logic Update:** Configure the BMS or charger controller to apply a temperature compensation coefficient (TCC) to the absorption voltage. A typical TCC for VRLA is -3.0 mV/°C/cell. For a 48V 32-cell string, a 10°C rise above 25°C would lower the absorption voltage by 10°C × 32 cells × 3.0 mV/°C/cell = 960 mV.
3.  **Clamp Constraints:** Set the BMS to clamp the temperature compensation between the room’s adjustable setpoints (18°C–26°C). 
    *   At 18°C, use the standard absorption voltage.
    *   At 26°C, apply the full compensation (e.g., -240 mV for a 48V string).
    *   Ensure the compensation does not drop below the float voltage threshold that would cause sulfation.
4.  **Equalization Adjustment:** Reduce the frequency of equalization cycles during sustained high-temperature periods (e.g., from monthly to quarterly) to avoid forced overcharge events that exacerbate dry-out.

**Measures It Relies On**
*   **Electrochemical Behavior:** Relies on the fundamental relationship that VRLA charge efficiency and gas recombination efficiency decrease with temperature. Lowering voltage at high temperatures minimizes the *net* water loss rate (production minus recombination), counteracting the Arrhenius acceleration of dry-out.
*   **Thermal Inertia & Setpoint Range:** Relies on the room’s 18-26°C range being sufficient to buffer against extreme outdoor peaks. By operating the battery at the cooler end of this range (e.g., setting HVAC to 18-20°C during hot seasons), the baseline temperature is lower, so the compensation adjustment required is smaller, preserving cell life.
*   **Sensor Accuracy:** Relies on accurate per-cell or per-string temperature measurement to prevent false compensation that could lead to undercharge (sulfation) or overcharge (dry-out).

**Why This Mitigates the Stated Failure**
The failure is "accelerated dry-out at sustained high ambient temperature." This mitigation directly addresses the *rate* of dry-out by reducing the thermodynamic drive for electrolysis. Since Arrhenius aging (Ea ~ 80 kJ/mol) means a small temperature increase causes a large rate increase, preventing the *overcharge* condition that compounds this temperature effect is more effective than just cooling. By combining the HVAC setpoint adjustment (cooling the room to 18-20°C) with voltage compensation, the electrolyte is kept at a stable hydration level, halting the capacity fade between equalizations.
