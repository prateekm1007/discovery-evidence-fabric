**Mitigation Proposal: Shift-Defoamer Dosing Strategy**

**Mechanism:**
The core mechanism is to decouple the defoamer dosing from its current (likely fixed or continuous) schedule and instead align it with the specific time windows when sludge carryover and foaming occur. By re-scheduling the dosing to inject the chemical *just before* and *during* peak foam formation events—triggered by high organic load—rather than maintaining a steady baseline, the chemical is applied only when it is physically needed to break the surface tension of the foam. This prevents the foam from growing thick enough to breach the weir, thereby retaining the solids within the clarifier basin and protecting the effluent quality.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Identify Foam Onset:** Utilize existing level sensors at the weir or visual/automated foam detection (if available) to identify the precise time windows during the diurnal or loading cycle when foam begins to rise.
2.  **Re-schedule Pump Duty:** Modify the spray-defoamer dosing pump’s controller settings from "continuous" or "fixed periodic" to "event-driven" or "time-based intervals" that coincide with the identified foam onset windows. For example, if foam consistently appears 30 minutes after the high-load influent surge, the pump is scheduled to dose for 15 minutes starting at T+20 minutes.
3.  **Dose Rate Adjustment:** Ensure the pump’s flow rate is calibrated to deliver a sufficient volume to break the existing foam layer within the dosing window, rather than just maintaining it.

**Measures Relied On:**
*   **Temporal Correlation of Foam and Load:** The mitigation relies on the operational assumption that foaming is directly correlated with high organic load events (as stated in the problem), making time-based prediction of foam onset reliable.
*   **Pump Precision:** It assumes the spray-defoamer dosing pump can be accurately re-scheduled to start and stop within specific time windows without significant lag.
*   **Cost Optimization via Reduced Volume:** It assumes that by limiting dosing to only the critical foam periods, the total volume of defoamer used per day will decrease compared to continuous dosing, thereby reducing the operational cost (since defoamer is priced per litre).

**Operational Cost Statement:**
*   **Cost Impact:** **Net Reduction in Operational Cost.**
*   **Reasoning:** While the defoamer is expensive per litre, its current continuous usage is inefficient because it is being applied during times when no foam is present (wasteful) and potentially under-dosed during peak times (ineffective). By re-scheduling to target only peak foam windows, the total litres consumed per day are expected to drop significantly. Although the cost *per litre* remains fixed, the *total daily expenditure* decreases because fewer litres are required to achieve the same (or better) effluent quality. The savings are derived from eliminating "background" dosing that contributes little to foam control during low-load periods.
