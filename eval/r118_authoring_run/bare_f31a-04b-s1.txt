**Mitigation Proposal**

**Mechanism:**
Implement a **time-synchronized, closed-loop "Setpoint Limiting" strategy** for the caustic dosing pump. The system uses the *measured* MF inlet pH (as a supervisory alarm and event logger only, not a control input) to validate the effectiveness of the retuned setpoint, while the control action itself relies solely on the **dosing-pump stroke rate** modulated by a **feedforward flow-proportional (flow-scaled) ratio controller** with a strict upper bound. The core mitigation is to retune the pump setpoint profile from a constant value to a **flow-scaled ramp with a hard ceiling**, preventing the integral windup and overshoot that caused pH spikes.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Retune Setpoint Logic:** Replace the fixed caustic dosing setpoint with a **flow-scaled setpoint**. The target dosing rate (mL/min) is calculated as `Dose_Setpoint = k * (MF_Flow_Rate / Reference_Flow_Rate)`, where `k` is a calibrated gain representing the base dose at reference flow. This ensures that as feed water flow increases, the dose proportionally increases to maintain consistent pH, and as flow decreases, the dose decreases, preventing relative overdose.
2.  **Apply Stroke Rate Ceiling:** Program the pump controller with a **maximum stroke rate limit** (e.g., 80% of pump mechanical max) that corresponds to a theoretical maximum pH of ~9.8 (safely below 10.5). The controller logic is:
    `Actual_Stroke_Rate = min( Calculated_Stroke_Rate_from_kPI, Maximum_Stroke_Rate_Limit )`
    The PI term is driven only by the **feedforward flow signal** and a **pH deviation from target** if the pH probe is *stable* (drift compensated), but per constraint, we *do not* use pH as control input. Instead, we use **flow rate** as the primary process variable. The "retuned setpoint" is the `k` gain and the `Maximum_Stroke_Rate_Limit`.
3.  **Add a "Reset to Baseline" Safeguard:** Every 2 hours, or upon a flow drop-off >10%, the pump stroke rate is forced to a **minimal base rate** (e.g., 20% of `Maximum_Stroke_Rate_Limit`) for 30 seconds to flush any residual caustic concentration gradients in the dosing line, preventing accumulation and subsequent overshoot when normal flow resumes. This uses only stroke rate and time/flow signals, no pH input.

**Measures it Relies On:**
*   **Primary Control Input:** MF Feed Water Flow Rate (from a reliable flow meter, not pH probe).
*   **Control Variable:** Caustic Dosing Pump Stroke Rate (the only permitted actuator).
*   **Retuned Parameter:** The gain factor `k` (dose per flow unit) and the `Maximum_Stroke_Rate_Limit` (absolute ceiling on stroke rate).
*   **Supervisory Signal (Non-Controlling):** MF Inlet pH is monitored via a **drift-compensated logger** (using a 24-hour average or median filter to smooth out probe noise/drift) **exclusively** for:
    *   Triggering operator alarms if pH > 10.0 (early warning).
    *   Validating that the new flow-scaled setpoint keeps pH within 9.0-9.8 range over a 7-day trend.
    *   *Crucially, the pH signal is NOT in the control loop. It does not adjust stroke rate in real-time.*
*   **Timing:** A time-base for the "Reset to Baseline" flush.

**Why This Works:**
The original failure was "overshoot," implying that the previous control (likely a PI loop on pH or a constant setpoint) had too much gain or lacked feedforward, causing the pump to pump excess caustic when flow varied. By:
1.  Using **flow-scaled dosing**, the dose automatically matches the dilution capacity of the water, eliminating the most common cause of relative overdose (low flow + constant dose).
2.  Imposing a **hard stroke rate ceiling**, we guarantee that even if the flow signal is faulty or the PI calculation errors out, the pump *cannot* physically dose enough caustic to push pH above 10.5. The ceiling is calculated from first principles: `(Max_Allowed_pH - Base_pH) * Feed_Flow * MW_Caustic / (Density * Time) <= Max_Stroke_Rate * Pump_Rated_Dose`.
3.  Using **time-based flushing**, we prevent static accumulation in dead legs.

This mitigation strictly adheres to the constraint: only stroke rate is the control variable, pH is not used for control, and the setpoint is retuned via flow-scaled gain and rate ceiling.
