**Mitigation Proposal: Controlled Metering of the Existing Manual Grease Route**

**Mechanism:**
The failure is caused by "washover," which is the excessive volume of grease forcing the fluid portion (oil) out of the rolling element contact area, leading to localized starvation, increased friction, and subsequent spalling of the outer race. The mitigation mechanism is **volume restriction and thermal management**. By strictly limiting the grease quantity per interval to match the bearing’s lubrication volume rather than filling the housing to saturation, the excess oil is prevented from washing out the remaining grease. This maintains a stable grease film structure and reduces churning heat, which accelerates grease breakdown and race spalling.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Quantify and Standardize:** Calculate the required grease charge for the specific bearing housing based on the manufacturer’s recommended volume (typically 50% of the free space in the bearing housing). Do not fill until the grease "bleeds" or stops accepting load.
2.  **Modify Application Procedure:** Instead of continuous or "until full" application, the maintenance technician must use a **metering grease gun** or manually count strokes if a precise gun is unavailable. Each application interval must consist of a fixed number of strokes (e.g., 5–8 strokes, calibrated to volume) rather than open-ended pumping.
3.  **Frequency Adjustment:** Reduce the greasing frequency to the minimum effective interval determined by load and speed, ensuring the grease film is maintained without accumulation. For high-speed screen deck applications, this often means greasing *less* frequently but in smaller, precise quantities.
4.  **Inspection Protocol:** Implement a visual inspection step *before* each application to check for grease expulsion from the seal. If grease is seen weeping or spilling, the next application interval must be extended until the excess is naturally consumed or expelled, preventing further washover.

**Measures Relied On:**
*   **Reliance on Manual Discipline:** The solution depends entirely on the operator’s ability to count strokes or use a metered gun consistently, eliminating the "more is better" misconception.
*   **Existing Grease Spec:** It uses the fixed OEM grease pack, so no chemical change is made; the mitigation is purely procedural and volumetric.
*   **Seal Integrity:** It assumes the existing seals are functional enough to retain the correctly metered volume; if seals are damaged, the mitigation will fail regardless of metering accuracy.
*   **No Hardware Changes:** No automatic lubricators or new hardware are introduced, adhering to the constraint.
