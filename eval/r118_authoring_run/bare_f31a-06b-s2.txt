**Mitigation Proposal: Integrated Electrodeposition-Suppressed Caustic Cleaning with In-Situ Alkaline Phosphatase Activity**

**Mechanism:**
The failure arises from the interplay between calcium/magnesium carbonate and phosphate scale formation and the insufficient oxidative/chemical strength of the caustic CIP to solubilize the adherent hard-water deposits, leading to localized overheating and plate blistering. The proposed mitigation involves **raising the NaOH concentration to the upper limit of 2.5 wt% at 75°C** to maximize saponification of fatty acids and hydrolysis of organic precursors, while simultaneously introducing **non-toxic, food-grade alkaline phosphatase (ALP)** to the CIP circuit. ALP catalyzes the hydrolysis of insoluble calcium phosphates and magnesium phosphates within the scale matrix at high pH and temperature, effectively loosening the crystalline structure that mechanical agitation and standard caustic alone cannot penetrate. This dual-chemical approach prevents the "loss of bite" by ensuring that each cycle actively dismantles the inorganic matrix before the next acid descalc, maintaining a clean heat-transfer surface and preventing thermal stress.

**Implementation:**
1.  **CIP Circuit Modification:** Install a dedicated dosing line for the ALP enzyme into the CIP return loop, upstream of the spray ball or plate washers, ensuring it is injected only during the caustic phase.
2.  **Parameter Adjustment:** Program the CIP controller to increase the NaOH concentration from its current value to exactly **2.5 wt%** (the gasket-rated maximum) and maintain the temperature at **75°C** for the full caustic cycle duration.
3.  **Enzyme Dosing:** Dose the ALP at a concentration of 0.5–1.0 mL/L of CIP solution during the 75°C, 2.5 wt% NaOH phase. Ensure the enzyme is sourced from a food-grade supplier (e.g., *Candida antarctica* origin) and verify it remains stable at pH > 13 and 75°C for the cycle duration.
4.  **Cycle Sequence:** Execute the following sequence: Rinse → **Caustic (2.5 wt% NaOH + ALP) at 75°C** → Rinse → Acid Descal → Rinse. The acid step is retained for final neutralization and removal of any residual loose minerals but is no longer the primary tool for scale detachment.

**Measures Relied Upon:**
*   **Gasket Compatibility Verification:** The system’s gaskets are explicitly rated for **2.5 wt% NaOH at 75°C**. This measure ensures that the maximum allowed chemical and thermal stress will not cause seal failure or degradation, making the concentration increase safe within the hardware limits.
*   **Enzyme Stability Data:** Reliance on published stability profiles of food-grade ALP, which confirm activity at high temperatures (>70°C) and alkaline conditions (pH 12–14), provided the concentration is within the operational window. This ensures the biological component remains functional in the harsh CIP environment.
*   **Scale Morphology Knowledge:** Reliance on the understanding that hard-water scale in pasteurizers is primarily calcium and magnesium salts of carbonate and phosphate. The combined action of high-concentration NaOH (swelling organic binders) and ALP (hydrolyzing phosphate bonds in mineral matrix) is specifically targeted at this type of deposit, explaining why standard caustic "loses bite" without it.
