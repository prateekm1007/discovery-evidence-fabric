**Mitigation Proposal: High-Concentration Cyclic Caustic CIP with Thermal Shock Cycle**

**Mechanism:**
The core issue is that hard-water scale (primarily calcium carbonate and magnesium hydroxide) is forming a protective, low-thermal-conductivity layer on the pasteurizer plates. This layer insulates the plates, leading to localized hot spots that cause "blisters" (delamination or warping due to uneven thermal expansion). Simultaneously, the standard caustic wash is ineffective ("losing bite") because the scale layer physically blocks the NaOH from reaching the protein/fat residues and the metal surface, and the concentration/temperature is insufficient to dissolve the mineral scale between dedicated acid cycles. By raising the NaOH concentration to the maximum allowable limit and increasing the cycle frequency/temperature, we can chemically attack the organic matrix and begin dissolving the scale, while the thermal shock helps mechanically crack the scale layer, exposing it to the caustic.

**Implementation:**
1.  **Concentration Increase:** Raise the CIP caustic concentration from the current baseline to **2.5 wt% NaOH**, which is the upper limit of the gasket rating. This maximizes the chemical "bite" without exceeding safety limits.
2.  **Temperature Optimization:** Ensure the caustic spray temperature is maintained at **75°C** (the upper gasket rating). Higher temperatures increase the reaction rate for scale dissolution and organic cleaning.
3.  **Frequency & Duration:** Implement a **shorter-interval, high-intensity caustic cycle** between acid descals. For example, instead of a single daily caustic wash, run two 15-minute caustic cycles at 2.5% NaOH and 75°C with 10-minute soak periods, separated by a water rinse, before the next scheduled acid descacle.
4.  **Thermal Shock Sequence:** Modify the CIP program to include a rapid temperature drop from 75°C to ambient (using cold water rinse) immediately after the caustic spray/soak phase, followed by a hot acid shot if the cycle continues, or a final hot rinse. This thermal cycling induces mechanical stress on the scale layer, causing it to crack and flake off, thereby exposing fresh surface area for the next caustic wash.

**Measures Relied On:**
*   **Gasket Rating Compliance:** The solution relies on the fact that the gaskets are explicitly rated for 2.5 wt% NaOH at 75°C, ensuring no chemical or thermal degradation of seals occurs during the intensified cycle.
*   **Synergistic Cleaning Effect:** It relies on the synergistic effect of high-concentration caustic at high temperature to break down the organic binding agents within the scale matrix, making the mineral scale more susceptible to mechanical removal via thermal shock and subsequent acid descals.
*   **Reduced Heat Load Variance:** By maintaining consistent high-temperature caustic application, it reduces the thermal gradients across the plates, thereby mitigating the uneven expansion that causes blistering. The mechanical removal of insulating scale also restores uniform heat transfer, reducing the risk of local overheating.
