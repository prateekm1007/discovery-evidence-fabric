The primary mechanism governing the performance of nickel-based spinel oxides in alkaline OER is the surface redox conversion of the underlying oxide lattice into an active oxyhydroxide layer (NiOOH) during operation. The catalytic activity is not intrinsic to the stable bulk spinel phase but rather emerges from the reversible interconversion between Ni(II)/Ni(III) and Ni(III)/Ni(IV) species on the surface, a process that is kinetically limited by the ease of proton/electron transfer and the structural ability of the lattice to accommodate the associated lattice expansion. Therefore, optimizing the material requires decoupling the roles of the bulk and surface: the bulk spinel structure must provide long-term structural integrity to prevent dissolution and aggregation, while the surface must maintain a high density of accessible, catalytically active Ni sites with minimal kinetic barriers for the O–O bond formation step.

To exploit this mechanism, synthesis conditions should be optimized to create a "core-shell" or defect-rich architecture where the bulk preserves phase purity to ensure stability, but the surface is intentionally disordered or cation-deficient to facilitate the rapid formation of the active NiOOH layer. Specifically, synthesis temperatures and durations should be tuned to minimize excessive sintering, which lowers surface area and blocks active sites, while avoiding incomplete crystallization, which leads to amorphous phases prone to rapid dissolution. By employing data-driven approaches to map the parameter space of precursor pH and calcination temperature, one can identify a window where the cation distribution is slightly inverted relative to the ideal spinel, creating Ni vacancies or mixed-valence states near the surface. This engineered defect landscape lowers the activation energy for the surface redox transition and stabilizes the oxyhydroxide layer against passivation, thereby maximizing turnover frequency without compromising the structural backbone that ensures durability under harsh alkaline conditions.
