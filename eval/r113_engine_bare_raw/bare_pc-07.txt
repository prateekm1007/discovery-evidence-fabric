Hypothesis: The mechanism involves the overexpression of folate receptors (specifically FR-alpha) on the surface of lung carcinoma cells, which are significantly upregulated compared to healthy fibroblasts. To exploit this, we conjugate a high-affinity squaraine dye, acting as the photosensitizer, to folic acid. The resulting conjugate selectively binds to the abundant folate receptors on the cancer cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis, facilitating intracellular accumulation within the tumor. Once internalized, the squaraine component remains available to generate reactive oxygen species (such as singlet oxygen) upon irradiation with specific wavelengths of light. This targeted delivery ensures that the cytotoxic photodynamic damage is localized primarily to the carcinoma cells, while the minimal expression of folate receptors on surrounding healthy fibroblast tissue prevents significant binding and uptake, thereby preserving the therapeutic index and minimizing off-target toxicity.
