The core mechanism governing the solubility behavior of this amorphous copolymer in a ternary system is the competition between polymer-solvent enthalpy interactions and the specific hydrogen-bonding network formed between the amine/imine functional groups and the solvent components. Because the polymer is amorphous, crystallization kinetics are absent, meaning phase separation is driven purely by thermodynamic stability limits and diffusion kinetics. The amine and imine groups act as strong hydrogen bond acceptors and donors, creating specific, non-ideal interactions with the good solvent and the nonsolvent. Specifically, the imine groups can engage in strong dipole-dipole interactions or specific hydrogen bonding with polar solvent components, while the amine groups provide steric hindrance and additional hydrogen bonding sites. This complex interaction landscape shifts the binodal and spinodal curves in the ternary phase diagram compared to a simple PLA backbone, often expanding the metastable zone.

To exploit this mechanism for stable nanoprecipitation, one must model the free energy of mixing using a Flory-Huggins-type thermodynamic framework but augmented with a specific interaction parameter term that accounts for the hydrogen-bonding affinity between the amine/imine moieties and the solvent pairs. The hypothesis is that stability during nanoprecipitation is maximized when the initial composition of the ternary system is placed within the metastable zone but sufficiently close to the spinodal curve that nucleation is rapid and homogeneous, preventing the long-term instability associated with deep metastable regions where Ostwald ripening or secondary aggregation might occur. Practitioners should exploit this by mapping the modified ternary phase diagram experimentally or via computational thermodynamics, identifying a "processing window" where the diffusion rate of the nonsolvent into the polymer solution exceeds the rate of polymer-polymer association. By tuning the ratio of the good solvent to the nonsolvent to align with the shifted binodal curve, one can control the critical nucleus size and ensure that the amine/imine functionalities remain solvated just long enough to allow uniform phase separation, thereby producing nanoparticles with consistent size distribution and surface charge dictated by the exposed basic groups.
