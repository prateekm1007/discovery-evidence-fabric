The core hypothesis is that improved performance in laboratory tests for perovskites is often driven by extrinsic interfacial engineering and transient defect passivation rather than intrinsic bulk transport properties, and the mechanism of reliable distinction lies in decoupling the bulk material from the device architecture through comparative heterostructure analysis and time-resolved spectral dynamics.

To exploit this, researchers must move beyond single-cell benchmarks and implement a systematic "deconvolution protocol." First, isolate the bulk material by synthesizing identical perovskite films with varying electrode configurations (e.g., transparent conductive oxide vs. metal contacts) and measuring photovoltaic or sensing responses; any significant divergence in performance metrics like open-circuit voltage or transient response that correlates with contact type indicates electrode-mediated artifacts, such as energy-level alignment effects or contact-induced recombination. Second, employ time-resolved photoluminescence and terahertz conductivity on planar, electrode-free films to establish baseline intrinsic charge carrier lifetimes and mobilities, then compare these directly to bulk-boundary-dominated metrics from full devices. 

Crucially, this must be coupled with controlled degradation studies where only the electrode or interface layer is altered while keeping the bulk composition and deposition method constant. If performance retention or enhancement persists across multiple architectures but disappears when a specific interface layer is removed, the effect is likely extrinsic. Conversely, if performance scales linearly with film thickness according to intrinsic transport theory, the bulk properties are the dominant factor. This approach exploits the principle that intrinsic properties are architecture-invariant, whereas artifacts are architecture-dependent, allowing researchers to attribute gains to the crystal chemistry itself only when the mechanism remains consistent across orthogonal device configurations.
