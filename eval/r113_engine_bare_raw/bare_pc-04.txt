The core mechanism driving sluggish HOR kinetics and CO poisoning in alkaline environments is the strong adsorption of intermediates on platinum surfaces, which blocks active sites and raises the activation energy for the Volmer-Heyrovsky steps. To accelerate kinetics while enhancing CO tolerance with reduced noble metal loading, the hypothesis posits that engineering a specific electronic modification at the metal-support interface is superior to bulk alloying. Specifically, by anchoring isolated iridium single atoms or sub-nanometer clusters onto nitrogen-doped carbon supports, one can create a strong metal-support interaction that modulates the d-band center of the iridium. This electronic shift weakens the adsorption energy of CO relative to hydrogen, thereby lowering the barrier for CO desorption or its further oxidation, while simultaneously optimizing the hydrogen adsorption free energy ($\Delta G_{H^*}$) closer to the thermoneutral point required for rapid HER/HOR activity.

Exploiting this mechanism involves synthesizing catalysts where iridium atoms are atomically dispersed within the carbon lattice, utilizing the nitrogen dopants as anchoring sites to prevent iridium aggregation. This single-atom configuration maximizes the atom efficiency of the noble metal, drastically reducing the overall loading compared to bulk nanoparticles. The synergy arises because the doped carbon support does not merely act as an inert backbone; it electronically interacts with the iridium sites, creating a bifunctional pathway. In this system, the iridium sites handle the hydrogen oxidation efficiently, while the nitrogen-doped carbon and associated oxygen functionalities can facilitate the direct removal of any remaining CO species or prevent their initial strong binding due to the modified local charge density. Therefore, the path to high performance lies not in increasing the quantity of metal, but in precise atomic-scale positioning to tune the interfacial electronic structure, achieving a balance where hydrogen binds sufficiently weakly for fast turnover but CO binds weakly enough to be easily expelled, all within a highly concentrated single-atom architecture.
