The hypothesis is that the synthesis of a stable three-dimensional iron selenide framework can be achieved by leveraging a "charge-balancing and lattice-strain mitigation" mechanism, where mixed cations—specifically combining a large, low-charge alkali or alkaline-earth cation with a smaller, higher-charge transition metal cation—stabilize the 3D topology against collapse into lower-dimensional chains or layers.

In traditional iron selenides, the strong covalent character of the Fe-Se bond and the specific coordination preferences of Fe2+/Fe3+ often favor linear or planar arrangements to optimize orbital overlap and minimize electron-electron repulsion, leading to 1D or 2D structures. To overcome this, one must introduce steric and electrostatic constraints that force the Fe-Se polyhedra to interconnect in the third dimension.

The mechanism relies on two coupled effects:
1.  **Electrostatic Stabilization:** By introducing a secondary cation (e.g., Mg, Ca, or K) into the lattice, the overall charge balance is altered. The larger cation occupies interstitial sites within the 3D framework, providing electrostatic repulsion that prevents the collapse of the structure into dense layers or chains. Simultaneously, the mixed valence state of the transition metals (e.g., Fe2+/Fe3+) can be tuned to enhance the ionic contribution to the metal-anion bonding, which favors higher coordination numbers necessary for a 3D network.
2.  **Lattice Strain Engineering:** The size mismatch between the different cations creates local strain that distorts the FeSe4 tetrahedra. This distortion breaks the symmetry that typically allows for easy slippage or folding into 1D/2D motifs. The mixed cation composition creates a rigid, interlocking "cage" structure where the large cations lock the anionic sublattice in place, while the transition metals provide the covalent framework.

To exploit this mechanism, one should synthesize compounds with the general formula $A_{x}Fe_{y}Se_{z}$ where A is a large alkali or alkaline earth metal (K, Rb, Cs, Ca, Ba) and y is tuned to allow for mixed valency. The synthesis route should involve solid-state reactions under high pressure or high-temperature flux conditions to overcome kinetic barriers. Specifically, starting from precursors that favor the target stoichiometry, one should use a sealed quartz tube or high-pressure cell to suppress volatile selenium loss and enforce dense packing. The key is to select the A cation to be large enough to enforce a 3D interpenetrating network but small enough to fit into the interstices without causing catastrophic lattice instability. Characterization via single-crystal X-ray diffraction should reveal a 3D connectivity index of >2 for the Fe-Se bonds, confirming the departure from chain or layer structures. Magnetic measurements would then show coupled 3D exchange interactions, distinct from the anisotropic magnetism of 1D/2D analogues, validating the structural transformation.
