My hypothesis is that 1,3-azoles can be synthesized sustainably by utilizing specific Deep Eutectic Solvents (DESs) as both the green reaction medium and the catalyst, leveraging the 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition mechanism to exploit the unique hydrogen-bonding and ion-dipole interactions of the DES.

The mechanism involves the thermal or microwave-assisted [3+2] cycloaddition between a 1,3-dipole (such as a diazo compound or azomethine ylide) and a dipolarophile (an alkene or alkyne). I propose exploiting the fact that DESs, composed of hydrogen bond donors (like choline chloride or amino acids) and hydrogen bond acceptors (like organic acids or polyols), create a polar, non-covalent environment that stabilizes the transition state of the cycloaddition more effectively than water or traditional solvents. By selecting a DES with a low melting point and high thermal stability, the reaction can be performed at moderate temperatures without pressurized reactors. Furthermore, because DESs are derived from biodegradable components and have negligible vapor pressure, they do not contribute to volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions. The key to sustainability lies in the post-reaction phase: instead of using energy-intensive distillation to recover expensive ionic liquids, the DES can be separated from the product via simple extraction with a benign, low-toxicity solvent (like ethyl acetate or even water, depending on the DES composition) or by direct crystallization. This allows the DES to be dried and recycled multiple times without significant decomposition or toxicity buildup, thereby solving the issues of cost, toxicity, and waste associated with traditional methods while maintaining the high regioselectivity and yields characteristic of the 1,3-dipolar mechanism.
